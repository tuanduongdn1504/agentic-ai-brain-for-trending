"The world is rushing to build AI systems. It is not engineering them."

The mission: establish AI engineering — building efficient, reliable, safe, robust intelligent systems that operate in the real world, not just models in isolation — as a discipline alongside software and computer engineering. A physics-first, quantitative-reasoning textbook in the Hennessy & Patterson tradition: it teaches the layer underneath the tools — why an LLM feature costs what it costs, stalls where it stalls, and fails how it fails.

01 The organizing insight

Constraints drive architecture. The same model, algorithm, and data demand radically different engineering on a data center vs. a phone vs. a microcontroller — not by preference, but because different physics governs each environment (speed of light, thermodynamics, memory-movement energy). The lens is D·A·M: "AI capability is not a software feature; it is an emergent property of the co-design of Data, Algorithm, and Machine under statistical and computational constraints."

Four deployment paradigms span nine orders of magnitude in power and memory: Cloud (≈3 MW / TB) → Edge (≈200 W) → Mobile (≈5 W) → TinyML (≈50 mW / KB). "The engineering principles governing one end of the spectrum barely apply at the other."

03 The double deep dive — knowledge that transfers

What makes ML systems different

  • Software 2.0 / data-as-code — the dataset is the source code; training is "compilation." Debugging moves from stack traces to dataset inspection.
  • Failures are silent, not loud — models degrade as the world drifts (code unchanged) → continuous monitoring + retraining is mandatory infrastructure.
  • The 5–95 rule — ML code is ~5% of a production system; data pipelines, serving, and monitoring are ~95% (and where it fails). "ML is easy; ML Systems are hard."
  • The Verification Gap — a 224×224 image has 256^150,528 configurations; ImageNet covers ~1.4M. Exhaustive pre-deployment testing is impossible.
  • Iteration velocity beats initial accuracy; constraints found at launch cost orders of magnitude more than at day 1 → propagate them backward.

The quantitative laws (the physics)

Iron Law of training

T = O / (R_peak × η_hw)

Peak FLOP/s is marketing; realized MFU is the binding constraint (GPT-3 ≈ 0.45).

The memory wall

move a bit ≈ 100–10⁶× compute-a-bit

"Arithmetic is nearly free; memory access is expensive." Cost rises as data leaves the chip.

Amdahl / acceleration wall

Speedup = 1 / ((1−p) + p/G)

10% serial work caps an infinite accelerator at 10× total speedup.

KV-cache is the LLM serving wall

Llama-3-8B ≈ 128 KB/token → 16.8 GB @128k ctx

More than the 16 GB of weights — a 24 GB GPU OOMs. Cache grows linearly with ctx × batch.

Serving dominates training

~$2M train vs ~$18M/yr serve @1M DAU

100–1000×. Every 1% efficiency saves ongoing cost for the model's life.

Little's Law headroom

p99 explodes above ~70% utilization

Keep 40–60% headroom; don't saturate accelerators.

INT8 is co-design

4× throughput — only on Tensor Cores / NPUs

~1.5–2× on a generic CPU. The gain materializes only when both layers were designed together.

Fleet MTBF cascade

100 nodes ≈ 10 hr · 25K GPUs ≈ 2 hr

At scale, failure is routine operational state — checkpoint, timeout barriers, watchdog.

Governance & sustainability

Fairness is a system constraint on a Pareto frontier (a measurable ~4pp accuracy tax), not a model property. Energy is a hard ceiling: GPT-3 training ≈ 1,287 MWh ≈ 120 US-household-years; carbon intensity varies ~40× by region → when and where you run is a first-class optimization lever.

04 Why this lands on hireui

The sharpest Goal-#2 content: responsible-AI-for-hiring

Amazon's recruiting tool learned to penalize résumés with "women's" because training data was male-dominated — and removing explicit gender didn't fix it: proxy variables (college, activities, career gaps) reconstructed gender silently. COMPAS was perfectly calibrated yet violated equalized-odds. Obermeyer showed cost-as-a-proxy inherits the bias of the system that made the proxy.

Lesson for hireui: removing protected attributes is insufficient — disaggregated per-group outcome monitoring is the only real defense. This is a textbook that is literally about your product's domain.
