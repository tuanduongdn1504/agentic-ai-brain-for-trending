# Donella Meadows / "Thinking in Systems" — source fact-check

## The garble

The video's Vietnamese auto-captions render a proper name as "Donelamedo" / "Donan Mido" and a concept as "điểm đoàn 7" / "điểm đoàn bảy" while describing the source material Codex draws on for the demo's "systems thinking" lesson.

## Resolution

Both garbles resolve cleanly to real, well-established sources:

- **Donella H. Meadows (1941–2001)** — American environmental scientist and systems-thinking pioneer; PhD in biophysics from Harvard; taught at Dartmouth for 29 years; lead author of the 1972 report *The Limits to Growth*.
- **"Thinking in Systems: A Primer"** — Meadows' foundational systems-thinking book, drafted in 1993, edited posthumously by colleagues at the Sustainability Institute (Diana Wright) and published in 2008. An international bestseller, foundational to the field.
- **"Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System"** — Meadows' 1997 essay (*Whole Earth Catalog*, Winter 1997), presenting twelve leverage points — specific places in a system where small, strategic interventions produce disproportionately large effects. "điểm đòn bẩy" is the correct Vietnamese translation of "leverage point(s)."

## Verdict

**CONFIRMED.** This is well-established public knowledge, verified against Wikipedia, the book's publisher (Chelsea Green), and the Donella Meadows Project archive. The video's use of this material is a correct, faithful citation once the caption garble is corrected — the underlying claim was never in doubt, only the transcription.
