# Overview — Beyond the Harness / Adaptive Engineering

## Source

Rajiv Chandegra, *"Beyond the Harness: A Journey Towards Adaptative Engineering"* — [qdZzND79mcg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdZzND79mcg), AI Engineer Europe 2026 (London), 37:01. See [[source-provenance]].

## Who is speaking

- **Rajiv Chandegra** — a **practicing GP (medical doctor) in London** (~16 years' medical experience, Cholmley Gardens Medical Centre) who has "been doing some AI engineering for the past few years." Founder/director of **Annicha Labs** ([annicha.co](https://annicha.co/), [rajivchandegra.com](https://rajivchandegra.com/)) — a ventures/advisory firm applying technology to "complex challenges in the real world." X: [@rajivchandegra](https://x.com/rajivchandegra).
- His stated interest: **"multi-agent, multi-human, multi-institutional collaboration."** The medical/real-world lens is load-bearing for his argument — his examples of "the messy real world" are social and physical systems, not codebases.

## The thesis in one paragraph

Today's AI engineering steers agents with a **fixed harness** — system prompts, `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md`, tools, roles, sequencing, and (recently) loops — all decided **before** runtime. That model is reliable, auditable, and traceable; Chandegra calls it **"Taylorism for AI"** / the factory model, and says it is the *right* answer for **complicated** problems. But two forces break it: (1) models keep getting exponentially better, so any hand-built harness risks becoming outdated in weeks; and (2) AI is about to leave the screen for the **messy real world** — multi-agent, multi-human, multi-institutional, physical — which is **complex**, not merely complicated. A fixed harness is brittle there. His successor paradigm, **adaptive engineering**, keeps the engineer but relocates the work: you design only **constraints** (the "rules of play") and let the **harness itself emerge, stabilize, adapt, and dissolve at runtime** from local agent interactions — the way a flock emerges from birds following simple local rules. *"You don't build the harness anymore. You let the agents form the harness that best fits the environment in that moment."*

## The arc of the talk

1. **[00:04–02:58] Frame.** Current paradigm = use/build a fixed harness (Pi, Claude Code, Cursor, Codex) to steer stateless LLMs. Future differs in two ways (models outpace harnesses; real-world exposure). Coins **adaptive engineering**: harness emerges/adapts/dissolves mid-runtime; engineer designs the constraints.
2. **[02:58–07:20] The current paradigm.** Anatomy of a harness (vendor system prompt users can't modify; `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` loaded each session; tool calling; specialized agents with skills/roles; loops → outcome → human review). "Before running, the entire harness has been predetermined. That's harness engineering." **Harness definition: "the model is the engine and the harness is everything built around it to make that engine useful."** Hundreds of harnesses; differentiated by config **and** by an opinionated design philosophy.
3. **[07:20–10:42] Payoffs & the catch.** Fixed harness = reliable + auditable + linear causality; the factory line. But reliability is bought by **suppressing variance that novelty requires**. Determinism and emergence pull in opposite directions. "The factory method is the right answer to a fixed problem and the wrong answer to a moving problem."
4. **[10:42–16:49] What is the real world made of?** Two paradigms: **reductionist/analytical** (world = stable parts; relationships secondary — "the metaphysics of a factory") vs **relational/systems** (world = processes and relationships first; "a stable thing is a slow pattern in an ongoing flow… more like a flame than a crystal"). Emergence: **water's wetness**, **flocks of birds** (local rules → whole new order no part designed). Ackoff's **"mess"** = a system of interacting problems in motion. **Complicated** (jumbo jet, clock — analyze/plan/predict) vs **complex** (flock, market, organization — **probe, sense, respond**). "The most expensive mistake in modern design: treating a complex problem like a complicated one."
5. **[16:49–20:07] Complex-system ingredients.** Diverse agents (diversity = fuel) + local interaction (no agent sees the whole) + recursive learning/adaptation → **emergence** → settle into **attractors** (stable states, e.g. water at room temp). "Nobody's steering — it's self-organizing. That's the opportunity."
6. **[20:07–27:22] Adaptive engineering, defined.** *"The discipline of designing constraints to the extent that the harness emerges on its own, stabilizes, and adapts… in ways you could not specify in advance. The harness becomes the ongoing output rather than the input."* Simulation: isomorphic-but-undifferentiated agents, coupling builds until ~one connection per agent → a system emerges → specialization into **niches** (identity = position relative to others, not something you assigned) → emergent **clusters/boundaries the system drew** → conventions/norms → **"governance without a governor."** The engineer designs constraints and then **senses and responds**, rather than stopping and restarting.
7. **[27:22–32:30] It's a continuum.** Not binary; no purely fixed and no purely autonomous pole. Examples on the map: **Hermes AI** ("self-improving agent that creates skills from experience") = adaptive but **vertical** intelligence (smarter individuals); the talk's claim is that **horizontal** intelligence (how groups coordinate) is the higher-leverage frontier. **Pi** = adaptive **at the design stage** (minimal, maximally extensible) but not adaptive **at runtime**. Engineer's three tunable questions: **enable vs govern**, **reward cohesion vs cost deviation**, **how fast (rate of coupling)**. You can only *sense and respond* to the emergent harness, not hard-edit it.
8. **[32:30–37:01] Failure modes + takeaway.** Real risks: premature attractors without genuine **selection pressure** → **drift**; **monoculture** (agents all trained on same data); **legibility collapse**; no pre-runtime predictability. Closing thesis: as models improve and AI enters the real world, *"the limiting factor is not going to be the strength of the model. It's going to be the **adaptability of the harness**"* — which he equates with decentralized, mid-runtime multi-agent orchestration. Ends with an open invitation: "is anyone else exploring this? Please reach out."

## What this is and isn't

- **It IS:** a well-read, honest, forward-looking *design-philosophy* talk that names its own failure modes and explicitly frames the future as *speculative* ("rests on two assumptions").
- **It ISN'T:** a method, a tool, a benchmark, or a working implementation. There is **no code, no demo, no evaluation.** Every prescriptive claim ("let the harness emerge") is a direction, not a procedure. See [[caveats-and-corrections]] for the critic's four objections and [[vs-harness-engineering-corpus]] for why the vault's own evidence currently points the other way.

## Key takeaways

- The word "harness" is used in **exactly the corpus sense** — Chandegra's definition matches Lopopolo/Tejas Kumar almost verbatim. This is a genuine intra-field debate, not a terminology clash.
- The real move is **relocating** engineering effort from *specifying the structure* to *specifying the constraints* + *sensing and responding* to what emerges.
- **Horizontal > vertical intelligence** is the talk's one falsifiable(-ish) strategic bet: leverage comes more from agent-to-agent coordination than from smarter individual agents.
- It is a **counter-thesis** to this vault's entire harness-engineering thread — valuable precisely *because* it disagrees.
