# Anti-hype framing — "I like the tech, I hate the narrative"

## Source
Quân IT, [`RcF6ofU2nLs`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RcF6ofU2nLs) [00:04–01:56]. Verified claims: [[quanit-becoming-ai-engineer-2026/claims-scorecard|claims-scorecard]] (C1).

## The distinction he draws

He pre-empts the obvious objection ("your channel says AI is a bubble — why teach it?"):

- **What he does NOT reject — the technology.** LLMs, vector databases, embeddings, MCP: *"wonderful technologies… they will change everything we know later."*
- **What he rejects — the narrative-push.** Two named targets:
  1. CEOs pressuring everyone: *"use AI or you're fired."*
  2. The financial posturing: *"OpenAI saying it won't IPO for anything under $1 trillion."*
- His principle: **a technology that proves its value gets adopted on its own.** It doesn't need everyone forcibly pushing "AI everywhere."

## The blockchain analogy (his core rhetorical move)

He maps the current AI hype onto the blockchain cycle he lived through:

- Blockchain was force-fit into **metaverse, supply chain, agriculture, logistics.**
- Take product-provenance-on-chain: someone still has to *update* the ledger. If that human is slow, expensive, lazy, or lies, the chain records garbage. **The immutability of the ledger doesn't fix the honesty of the input** — a database could do the same job. → the technology didn't remove the human trust problem, it just relocated it. (This is a GIGO / oracle-problem argument, correctly applied.)
- Conclusion: hype-forced use-cases where the tech doesn't actually solve the core problem are the "stupid things happening around AI" he complains about — *not* the tech itself.

## The load-bearing verified fact

The narrative he calls absurd is **real, not a strawman** (this is the interesting verification result):

- **C1 — CONFIRMED.** Sam Altman publicly framed a sub-$1-trillion OpenAI IPO as a "nonstarter," and OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 targeting a ~$1T valuation (as of mid-2026). Sources in [[quanit-becoming-ai-engineer-2026/claims-scorecard|claims-scorecard]].
- So his *factual* claim ("OpenAI tied its IPO to ~$1T") checks out. Whether that is "absurd" is his **opinion** — a defensible one, but label it as opinion, not fact.

## Key Takeaways

- The framing is **pro-technology, anti-hype** — an important nuance; he is not a Luddite, he's a value-first pragmatist.
- The blockchain→AI analogy is a **real, non-trivial argument** (the oracle/GIGO problem), not just cynicism — hype-forced use-cases fail when the tech doesn't touch the actual bottleneck.
- His most quotable "absurd" claim is **factually accurate** (the $1T-IPO stance is real). Good instance of the [[quanit-becoming-ai-engineer-2026/caveats-and-corrections|discard-as-garble guard]] in reverse: a claim that *sounds* like hype-mockery turned out to be a verifiable event.
