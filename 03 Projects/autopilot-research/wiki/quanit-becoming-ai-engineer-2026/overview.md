## The framing

He opens with a disclaimer ([00:04–01:56]): despite his channel's frequent "AI is a bubble" tone, he has **no problem with the technology** — LLMs, vector databases, embeddings, MCP are "wonderful… they will change everything we know." What he hates is the **hype narrative**: CEOs threatening "use AI or you're fired," the "$1 trillion or no IPO" posturing, and the reflexive "AI everywhere." His analogy: the **blockchain bubble** (forced into metaverse / supply-chain / agriculture / logistics), where product-provenance-on-chain still needed a trusted human to keep the ledger honest — so the tech solved nothing the humans didn't. Good technology proves its value and gets adopted; it doesn't need everyone pushing a narrative. See [[quanit-becoming-ai-engineer-2026/anti-hype-framing|anti-hype-framing]].

Then the payload: **you don't become an AI engineer by becoming a frontier researcher.** Those researchers — building DeepSeek/Kimi-class models at OpenAI/Meta/X — are a different, elite job. An AI engineer **builds systems and solutions on top of** the technology. Same as blockchain: you can build DeFi/NFT/GameFi without understanding proof-of-work internals.

## Key Takeaways

- The whole thesis: **AI engineer ≠ AI researcher.** You build *on top of* models; you don't build the models.
- The scarce skills are **not** the AI ones. By his estimate (via friends who switched jobs), only ~**30%** of the new skillset is AI-specific; ~**70%** is ordinary engineering. (See the MISLEADING/attribution note in [[quanit-becoming-ai-engineer-2026/caveats-and-corrections|caveats]].)
- **Data quality gates everything** — a policy document with 10 conflicting versions defeats *any* model, no matter how good.
- **Domain knowledge is the verifier** — because AI output is nondeterministic, you need enough domain expertise to know when the answer is wrong.
- **Build > watch.** Tutorials won't make you good; a real project you struggled through will — and it becomes the story that wins interviews.
