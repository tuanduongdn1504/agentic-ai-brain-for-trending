# The "$80K/Mo" Title and the Revenue Claims

## Verdicts
- Title "How I built an $80K/Mo mobile app" → **MISLEADING** (high confidence)
- "SimplyWise doing 60k/mo... another 60k... this one 80k/mo" → **MISLEADING** (high confidence): third-party estimates presented as fact, source never named

## Title forensics

- In-video, the $80K/mo belongs to an **unnamed competitor app** ("this one's doing 80k per month"); Track Rabbit — the app he actually builds — is never shipped, monetized, or submitted on camera, and no evidence was found that it ever was.
- Jason Lee's verifiable business is **content**: his own Creator Academy page self-reports **~$26K/month** from YouTube AdSense, courses, sponsorships, and affiliates (heyjasonlee.com/ca). Searches found **no App Store developer page or shipped app** under his name (the "Jason Lee Limited" developer account is a different entity).
- The title is a **channel formula**, not a one-off: "How I Vibe Coded a $400K/mo App" (`hDOUzlJwM1E`, 2026-04-24, 293K views — the subject app is CoinSnap, owned by Hong Kong company Next Vision Limited per Sensor Tower), "How I Vibe Coded a $900K App in 13 Minutes," "I sat on my couch and built a $100K app." The dollar figure is always the *cloned niche's estimated* revenue.
- Fair correction: an accurate title would be "How I cloned an $80K/mo app's feature set with Claude Code (demo)."

## The revenue numbers themselves

- **SimplyWise is real and substantial**: iOS App Store id1538521095, 4.9★ with ~36,000 ratings; SimplyWise Inc. founded 2020, ~$21.1–21.2M raised (incl. a $12M Series A, Oct 2025). At that funding level, $60K/mo (=$720K ARR) is *plausible* — but **no public source corroborates it**.
- App Store pages **do not display revenue**. The numbers almost certainly come from an estimate tool (Sensor Tower / Appfigures class). Known accuracy:
  - Appfigures self-reports **5–25% error** (MAPE methodology, best→worst case).
  - Industry writeups put typical tool accuracy at 20–30% for most apps; **no tool has actual store data**.
  - One documented case: Sensor Tower estimated **$40K MRR when actual was $90K** (55% under) — errors run both directions.
  - Sensor Tower discloses **no quantified error margins** publicly.
- Scale check: **Expensify**, the largest public receipt-scanning company, reported ~$142.1M TTM revenue in FY2025 (~$11.8M/mo for the whole company) — the niche is real, and $40–80K/mo boutique apps within it are credible as a *class*, just not verified as *these specific numbers*.
- What honest sourcing would look like: name the tool, label the numbers as estimates, state the margin of error.

## Why it still (sort of) holds up

The underlying niche thesis — boring B2B sub-niche, one painful problem, sticky data — is directionally reasonable and consistent with B2B SaaS benchmarks (~88–90% annual retention; switching costs 2–4× first-year fees). What's missing is any receipt-app-specific retention evidence, and the QuickBooks integration that anchors the "necessity not nice-to-have" pitch is deferred entirely ([[expo-go-to-app-store-gap]]).

## Key Takeaways
- Treat every dollar figure in this genre as an **unattributed estimate with ±25–55% observed error**, in both directions.
- The presenter's incentive structure is the funnel (ads, sponsor, newsletter, course), not app revenue — see [[source-provenance]].
- The niche-selection *method* (revenue-estimate tools + App Store category mining) is usable — with the error bars stated — as an idea-validation heuristic, not as a financial plan.
