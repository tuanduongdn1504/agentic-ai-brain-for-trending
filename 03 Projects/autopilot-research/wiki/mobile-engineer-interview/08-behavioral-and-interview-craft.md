# 08 — Behavioral & interview craft

Tuấn's behavioral section is **last, scenario-based, and judgment-focused** — he wants to see how you *think*, not virtue clichés. These recur across all five interviews.

## The standard questions (and how to land them)

- **Self-introduction / background.** Timeline + stack + what you actually built. Keep it to ~60–90s; lead with the most relevant experience. (See the separate spoken-delivery coaching if you're rehearsing this aloud.)
- **Strengths (name ~5).** Be specific with evidence: "initiative — I self-teach and pick up projects", "resilience — I debug independently then ask", not just adjectives. Candidates who gave examples scored better than ones who listed traits.
- **Weaknesses + plan.** Honest + a concrete improvement action ("responsive design is weak; it's improved across my last two projects"; "CS fundamentals — 30 min/day on algorithms"). Self-aware beats "I'm a perfectionist".
- **Career plan (1–2 yr and 5 yr).** The answer interviewers liked: **specialise first, then broaden** — e.g. "go deep in React Native, ship real features end-to-end, then add Kotlin/Swift". Depth-before-breadth reads as mature.
- **English self-rating.** Honest calibration ("reading/docs good; speaking rusty; here's my plan/cert") beats over-claiming. These interviews run in Vietnamese, so it's self-report — don't inflate.
- **Questions for us.** Not "none". Ask about onboarding/ramp, mentorship, team structure, how juniors level up, code-review culture. Signals genuine interest and lets you evaluate fit.

## The scenario questions (where judgment shows)

- **A teammate isn't pulling their weight.** Strong answer: assume good intent first → **1:1 to understand the blocker** → clear expectation → document → escalate to lead/mentor if it continues. ⚠️ Weak version jumps straight to "remind them, then report them" — add the *understand-the-blocker* step.
- **Two teammates are in conflict.** Mediate: **listen to both, separate person from problem, decide on project goals (data/trade-offs), document, move on.** Don't just "let them talk it out".
- **Deadline clash (e.g. feature release vs thesis defense).** ⚠️ The common weak answer is "I'll pull an all-nighter". Strong answer leads with **communicate/negotiate**: flag the conflict to stakeholders early, negotiate scope/timeline, ask for help — *then* manage your own time. Ownership ≠ silent self-sacrifice.
- **Group-of-friends hiring / you might not all get in.** Mature answer: optimise for **individual fit**; the company has real constraints (N roles, N stacks); staying together matters less than the right opportunity. (The RN candidate nailed this.)
- **A job with no mentor, no roadmap, just tasks.** Show **self-rescue before escalation**: clarify goals with the manager, find peer mentors, self-teach from docs/videos, document learnings, escalate only when truly blocked. (Junior candidate stopped at "ask the supervisor" — add the self-driven layer.)

## Reading Tuấn (if he's your interviewer)

- He **teaches during the interview** — if he explains a concept (Set, spread, TS benefits), **restate it back correctly**; he rewards candidates who integrate feedback on the spot.
- He rewards **thinking out loud + self-correction** over confident-wrong recall. If unsure, reason toward the answer rather than bluffing.
- He probes **"why"** after every "what" — prepare one level deeper than the definition.
- He's **not gatekeeping trivia** — admitting "I haven't used X" honestly, then showing how you'd learn it, is a valid answer for a junior role.

## The meta-lesson

Across five candidates, the ones who did best weren't the ones who *knew the most* — they were **honest about gaps, reasoned out loud, and self-corrected**. For a junior/intern role, Tuấn is buying **growth trajectory + coachability**, and the behavioral round is where he measures it.

## Key Takeaways

- Behavioral is **judgment**, not virtue: lead scenarios with **communicate → understand → escalate**, not "work harder".
- **Specialise-then-broaden** is the career answer that lands.
- Be **honest about gaps + show the learning plan** — coachability is what a junior interview is buying.
- Always have **questions to ask**; restate anything the interviewer teaches you.
