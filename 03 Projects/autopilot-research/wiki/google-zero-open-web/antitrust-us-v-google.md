# US v. Google — Search Antitrust (through mid-2026)

## Source

- Liability: Judge Amit Mehta, US District Court DC, 2024-08-05 opinion (277pp; market shares 89.2% desktop / 94.9% mobile general search) — [analysis](https://www.purduegloballawschool.edu/blog/news/google-landmark-case)

## The rulings

| Date | Ruling |
|---|---|
| 2024-08-05 | **Liability**: Google illegally maintained monopolies in general search services and search text ads (Sherman Act §2). "Google is a monopolist, and it has acted as one to maintain its monopoly." |
| 2025-09-02 | **Remedies**: DOJ's Chrome divestiture **rejected**; Android divestiture rejected. Ordered: (1) **no exclusive** default-search distribution contracts for 6 years (Search/Chrome/Assistant/Gemini across Apple, Samsung, carriers…) — but **payments for non-exclusive placement remain legal**; (2) share certain search-index + user-interaction data with qualified competitors on commercial terms; (3) a Technical Committee oversees compliance for 6 years |

## Appeal state (mid-2026)

- Google appealed (2026-01-16), challenging data-sharing + oversight; **DOJ cross-appealed** (2026-02-03) still seeking Chrome divestiture
- Google's opening brief filed in the DC Circuit 2026-05-22; oral arguments expected late 2026/early 2027; final resolution likely 2027+
- Remedies apply pending appeal
- Precedent both sides cite: **US v. Microsoft** — DC Circuit vacated the breakup order (2001) while affirming liability; the historical rhyme favors behavioral-not-structural outcomes
- Parallel case: **ad-tech** (Judge Brinkema, EDVA) — liability found 2025-04-17 for publisher ad-server + exchange monopolization; remedy phase since Sept 2025
- EU track (separate; see [[licensing-resistance]]): DMA compliance proceedings opened 2026-01-27 (Android/Gemini interoperability; search-data sharing) + the Dec 2025 formal investigation into content use for AI

## Assessment of the video's characterization

"The penalties were soft. Google kept its browser, told to share some data and drop a few exclusive deals. Naturally, Google is appealing. The monopoly … practically stayed in place." — **CONFIRMED as fair.** The one nuance: the remedies are not nothing (data-sharing + non-exclusivity are real behavioral constraints), and *both* sides appealed — the DOJ still wants Chrome. The video omits the cross-appeal (it post-dates its 2024-2025 script sources).

## Key Takeaways

- The legal finding of monopoly is settled at district level and *not* what's mainly under appeal from Google's side — the fight is over remedies.
- Nothing in the remedies touches AI Overviews / AI Mode directly. The answer-machine pivot proceeds legally unimpeded in the US; the sharper constraints on AI-content use are coming from the EU and from private suits ([[licensing-resistance]]).
- Google can still buy default placement — just not exclusively. The distribution moat is dented, not drained.
- For timeline-citers: liability Aug 5 2024 → remedies Sept 2 2025 → cross-appeals Jan-Feb 2026 → briefs mid-2026 → arguments ~late 2026 → ruling 2027+.
