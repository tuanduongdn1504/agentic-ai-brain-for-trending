## Raptive's $2B model (all confirmed)

- **Raptive** = ad-management firm for ~5,000 creator/publisher sites. March 2024 (pre-AI-Overviews-launch, modeling SGE): industry-wide **~$2B/yr ad-revenue loss**; on its own network, modeled **~25% average traffic loss** (travel/family 29%, food 20%)
- **Marc McCollum** (title verified: EVP Innovation → **Chief Innovation Officer**; the VN dub's "Mark McCallum" is a name garble) told Digiday (2024-05-24) the shipped AI Overviews were a "**far more extreme version**" than the beta they modeled — $2B "may be the very low end"
- The video's "20–60% traffic falls" range traces to eMarketer's SGE-era synthesis — an industry consensus band, not a single named study (precision flag from the critic)
- Framing note: the $2B is a **model, not a measurement** — the video presents it approximately correctly ("modeled the impact … estimated")

## Germany — the measured version (video's vaguest claim turned out MOST verified)

- SISTRIX (Feb 2026, 100M+ German keywords): AI Overviews cost German publishers **~265 million organic clicks per month**
- AI Overviews on ~**20%** of German keywords; where present, **position-1 CTR drops 27% → 11%** (−59%)
- Wikipedia alone: −31.6M clicks/month. Overall: 6.6% of all German organic clicks — but **1–24% by industry** (informational content hardest; transactional barely touched)
- The video's "hundreds of millions of clicks from publishers each month" = accurate

## The aggregate 2025-2026 picture

- **Chartbeat** (2,500+ sites, via Reuters Institute): Google referral traffic **−33% globally, −38% US**, Nov 2024 → Nov 2025
- **Semrush news study**: organic visits to news sites 2.3B/month (mid-2024) → <1.7B (May 2025)
- **Digital Content Next** (member self-report, May-June 2025): median −10% YoY (news brands −7%, non-news −14%) — smaller than the scare numbers, real nonetheless
- **Reuters Institute survey (Dec 2025)**: media leaders expect a further **40%+ search-traffic decline over the next three years**
- **Layoffs (Q1 2026)**: Washington Post, Atlanta Journal-Constitution (~50 / 15%), Politico (~3%), Nexstar, Vox, CNBC, WSJ locals — attributed *in part* to search collapse (⚠️ single aggregator source MediaCopilot for the roll-up; the "WaPo one-third of staff" figure is **reported-only, not independently verified**; causation mixes search decline + print-ad decline + AI adoption)
- Cost asymmetry (the video's sharpest economic point, no correction needed): the *cost* of investigation/testing/reporting is unchanged while its funding channel evaporates

## Key Takeaways

- Both headline economics claims are fully confirmed — and they are different *kinds* of numbers: Raptive's $2B is a forward model; SISTRIX's 265M clicks/month is a backward measurement. The measurement (Germany) validates the model's direction.
- Impact is wildly uneven: informational/review content loses up to ~60% CTR; transactional queries are barely affected. A SaaS whose organic traffic is product/transactional (like hireui's) has *lower* direct exposure than publishers — but its content-marketing arm has full exposure.
- Median publisher decline (DCN −10%) vs worst-case (−60%+) — the honest range is wide; distrust any single number without its population.
- The layoff-wave attribution is the softest link in the chapter: real layoffs, mixed causes, one aggregator source.
