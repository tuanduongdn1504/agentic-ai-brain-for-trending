# Case study — Palafin's regional comeback (the Coaching-Sneasler engine)

> **Source:** [Youtuber này mang Palafin đánh giải !?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mKiPLETxFGk) — `mKiPLETxFGk`, **Sinistcha Latte** (Vietnamese), 2026-08-23, **19:12**. A cast of a **best-of-3 at the first Pokémon Champions regional**: **Colin Haer** (a Palafin team, reportedly *undefeated* at the event) vs **Alberto** (a Tyranitar / Corviknight sand-ish team).
> **A *match*, not a build** — like the topic's other case studies, this covers the games **as revealed on stream** (each side brings 4 of 6 per game). **No importable teamlist** — I won't invent the hidden slots. Mechanics adversarially fact-checked; **one caster slip corrected** (see Watch-out).
> **The topic's 4th case study, and its first Palafin.** The hook is a Pokémon almost no one uses in this format — **Palafin (Zero to Hero)** — made a real threat by **Coaching Sneasler**, the same support tech behind the [#1-ladder Floette team](floette-coaching-dual-mega-team.html).

## Why Palafin is the story

**Palafin** was a top pick in Regulation A/B of Scarlet/Violet, then fell "back to almost zero" for the rest of that game's life. It resurfaced at the **first Pokémon Champions regional** and — per the caster — went **undefeated**. Its gimmick is the ability **Zero to Hero**: Palafin starts in a weak **Zero Form** (base Attack **70**), and the instant it **switches out**, it permanently becomes **Hero Form** — base Attack **160**, a 650 base-stat-total monster — for the rest of the game. So the line is: **lead it, switch it out to "flip" it, then bring it back as a nuke.**

But Colin's Palafin isn't just a damage stick. The team pairs it with **Coaching Sneasler** — Coaching raises an **ally's Attack *and* Defense** by a stage each — so Sneasler pumps the physical attackers (Palafin's own **Bulk Up** on top) into things that hit harder *and* get harder to kill every turn. Add **Mega Froslass** (Snow Warning → **Aurora Veil**, which cuts physical *and* special damage in snow) and you get a physical-offense core that snowballs into an unbreakable wall if left alone.

## How the Bo3 played out

**Game 1 — the Coaching clinic (Colin).** Colin led **Palafin + Sneasler** (the "Tide Walker" duo), switched Palafin out into **Garchomp** to flip Hero Form, and — crucially — Alberto's switch *missed the target*, letting **Sneasler Coach Garchomp twice**. A **+2 Def / +2 Atk Garchomp** simply couldn't be broken: Alberto had brought **four physical attackers and no special attacker**, so Brave Bird / Wave Crash bounced off, and Garchomp Dragon-Clawed through the board. **Colin 1–0.**

**Game 2 — Alberto adjusts (Alberto).** Same leads, different plan: Alberto brought **Choice-Scarf Garchomp** in early for **Earthquake** pressure (an Earthquake threatens *both* of Colin's mons, so Sneasler couldn't freely Coach), and finally deployed **Mega Florges** for **special** damage that ignores Defense boosts. A **double Protect** from Colin failed on Palafin, and Alberto scored a **double KO on Garchomp + Palafin** before the Coaching snowball could start. **Alberto 1–1.**

**Game 3 — the Froslass swing (Colin).** Alberto led **Scarf Garchomp** to deny Coaching again, and it looked controlled — until **Mega Froslass** flipped it: in Snow, a **Blizzard** off an 8-HP Froslass (which had just *survived a critical hit*) **KO'd Garchomp and froze Corviknight**. With Corviknight iced and unable to act, Colin brought **Palafin** back, let **Sneasler Coach it + Bulk Up**, and swept — the final blow a **critical-hit** Jet Punch. **Colin 2–1, match.**

## Lessons (why it's a case study)

- **Coaching turns an attacker into a wall you can't out-damage — unless you bring special attackers.** Game 1 was lost at *team preview*: Alberto brought four physical mons into a **Bulk Up + Coaching** team, so every boosted-Defense turn compounded. His game-2/3 fix was exactly right — lead **Scarf Garchomp Earthquake** to deny the free Coaching turns and bring **Mega Florges** for special damage. (Same "you need a special breaker vs a boosted physical wall" lesson, mirrored.)
- **Palafin has to *leave* to become a threat.** Zero to Hero means the strong form only exists after a switch — so the whole line is built around pivoting Palafin out (into a Coaching turn) and bringing it back as a Hero-Form nuke. Read the switch and you blunt the whole plan.
- **Weather is a lever both ways.** Colin's **Mega Froslass Snow** (100% Blizzard + Aurora Veil) is a real win-con, but **Tyranitar Sand** overwrites it — the Snow plan only works while Tyranitar is off the field. Game 3 turned on Froslass getting its Blizzard *before* the Sand came back.
- **Ability trivia decided a game.** Alberto's Garchomp ran **Sand Veil**, so a game-2 **Dragon Claw missed** (evasion), keeping it alive at a pivotal moment — a reminder that the *ability line* (Sand Veil vs Rough Skin) is as much a read as the moveset.

## Watch-out (honest notes)

- **Partial, unofficial teams.** Each side only revealed 4 of 6 per game; Colin's sixth and Alberto's full six never appeared. Treat this as a **piloting/decision study**, not a copy-paste team — there's no importable list.
- **Corrected caster slip (independently checked, per the vault's wiki-verify rule):** the cast described Palafin's Wave Crash as boosted by "Mystic Water **and Adaptability**." **Palafin cannot have Adaptability** — its *only* ability is **Zero to Hero** (that's what enables Hero Form, the entire strategy). The extra power came from **Hero Form's 160 base Attack + Mystic Water STAB**, not Adaptability. (Also: Sand Veil is **+1 evasion stage** in sand, ~25%.)

## Key Takeaways

- **Palafin (Zero to Hero) is a real Champions threat again** — lead it, switch it out to reach **Hero Form (160 Attack)**, then bring it back for **Jet Punch / Wave Crash / Bulk Up**. It went undefeated at the first regional in Colin Haer's hands.
- **The engine is Coaching Sneasler** — +Atk/+Def to an ally turns **Garchomp** (or Palafin) into a defense-boosted attacker that snowballs; the same tech as the topic's [Floette coaching team](floette-coaching-dual-mega-team.html).
- **Beat a boosted physical wall with special attackers + hazard-free pressure** — Alberto's comeback plan was Scarf-Garchomp Earthquake (deny Coaching turns) + Mega Florges (special damage past Defense boosts).
- **Mega Froslass Snow is a swing win-con** (100% Blizzard + Aurora Veil), but lives and dies by whether **Tyranitar Sand** is on the field.
- **This is a decision study** — Colin won **2–1**; no importable list, teams are as-revealed, hidden slots unknown.
