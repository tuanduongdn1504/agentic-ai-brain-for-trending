## Identity: the terrain does two jobs at once

Regulation M-C brought **Mega Evolution and Terrain Wars back** to the format, and this team squeezes both edges out of a single choice: **Psychic Terrain**, set by **Choice-Scarf Indeedee (male)**.

- **It blocks all priority** — for grounded Pokémon on *both* sides. That's the load-bearing trick, because **Mega Baxcalibur's Speed is 87**: too fast to want Trick Room, too slow to outrun the meta, and normally easy to revenge-kill with **Aqua Jet / Sucker Punch / Bullet Punch / Fake Out**. Under Psychic Terrain none of those work, so the tank just… sits there and wins. As the creator puts it, "psychic terrain was the way to save Excalibur."
- **It turns Expanding Force into a two-target nuke.** A grounded Indeedee's **Expanding Force** in its own terrain gets **1.5× power *and* hits both opponents** — the "size spam" (auto-caption for *psychic spam*) that carried most of the games.

Wrapped around that: **snow** from **Alolan Ninetales** raises the Ice-type Baxcalibur's **Defense by 50%** (and arms **Aurora Veil**), and a **Psychic-Seed Sneasler** converts the terrain into a **+1 Sp. Def bump *and* an Unburden Speed-double** in one turn — the fastest thing on the field, there to Coach Baxcalibur or clean up.

## The pieces (how the engine fits together)

- **Mega Baxcalibur (Dragon/Ice, Thermal Exchange).** The win-con and the meat-shield — a **175-Attack, HP-115 tank** (BST 700) that "tanks so many hits from so many different things." **Thermal Exchange** is doubly good in a Fire-heavy meta: it **can't be burned**, and a **Fire hit *raises* its Attack** — so leading into **Charizard-Y** is a buff, not a threat. Its move logic: **Ice Shard** (priority) snipes the **Garchomp** that's **4× weak to Ice** off that huge Attack + STAB; **Glaive Rush** (120-BP Dragon signature) is the finisher — but it leaves you **taking double damage until your next move**, so it's an "end-all" click, not a spam (the faster alternative is **Scale Shot**, which the creator discusses but doesn't run); **High Horsepower** is the Steel-coverage Ground move; **Protect** for safety.
- **Indeedee, male (Psychic Surge, Choice Scarf).** The engine. It sets **Psychic Terrain** on entry (blocking priority + arming the whole plan) and immediately fires a **Choice-Scarf Expanding Force** that's **faster than almost anything and hits both foes**. It's also **Normal/Psychic**, so it's **immune to Ghost moves** — it hard-walls **Basculegion's Last Respects** and **Dragapult's** Ghost STAB, forcing them into weaker options. **Trick** (Scarf onto a foe) and **Hyper Voice / Energy Ball** round it out. *(The creator notes the meta split: **male** Indeedee = fast offensive Expanding Force; **female** = the bulkier Trick Room support. This team wants offense, so it's male.)*
- **Sneasler (Unburden, Psychic Seed).** The combo piece. In Indeedee's Psychic Terrain the **Psychic Seed** is consumed for **+1 Sp. Def**, and losing the item triggers **Unburden → double Speed** — a real, tournament-proven engine that makes Sneasler "the fastest thing in the format," outspeeding even Tailwind'd threats. Now itemless, its **Acrobatics doubles from 55 to 110 BP** (great into Staraptor / Rillaboom), with **Close Combat** + **Poison Jab** STAB and, crucially, **Coaching** to hand Baxcalibur **+1 Atk / +1 Def** so it survives a hit it otherwise wouldn't.
- **Alolan Ninetales (Snow Warning, Focus Sash).** The weather. **Snow raises Baxcalibur's Defense 50%** (Ice-type) and lets Ninetales throw up **Aurora Veil** (team-wide damage cut — snow-only). **Blizzard** is never-miss chip in snow, and **Freeze-Dry** is the anti-Water tech (super-effective on Water) into **Basculegion / Pelipper** rain. **Focus Sash** guarantees it lives one hit to get the snow/Veil down.
- **Gholdengo (Good as Gold, Life Orb).** The special breaker — **Make It Rain / Shadow Ball / Power Gem** coverage into the format's new Megas, with **Good as Gold** shutting off enemy status moves. "Nothing revolutionary," in the creator's words — just clean damage. *(One accuracy note the creator glosses: **Make It Rain is a spread move that self-drops Sp. Atk by 2 in Champions** — a fire-once-and-reposition nuke, not a spam button, same nerf noted on the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/godchu-pikachu-support-team|Godchu Gholdengo]].)*
- **Incineroar (Intimidate, Rocky Helmet).** The glue, with a twist: **Rocky Helmet** means every contact move into it costs the attacker **1/6 max HP** — so **Basculegion's Life-Orb Aqua Jet**, Mausholds, and other physical pests chip *themselves* onto other Pokémon's KO ranges. **Fake Out / Parting Shot** tempo, **Darkest Lariat / Flare Blitz** damage.

## Game plan

1. **Lead the terrain + the spam.** The default lead is **Indeedee + Sneasler**: Psychic Terrain locks off priority and Expanding Force nukes both slots, while Sneasler procs its Seed → Unburden and starts sweeping (Acrobatics/Close Combat) or Coaches Baxcalibur.
2. **Use terrain to *protect*, not just to attack.** Against priority-reliant teams (Aqua Jet Basculegion, Sucker Punch, Bullet Punch, Fake Out), keeping Psychic Terrain up is how the slow Baxcalibur stays alive — treat the terrain timer as a resource.
3. **Snow when you need the bulk.** Bring **Ninetales** to set snow + Aurora Veil before you commit Baxcalibur into a scary board; the +50% Ice Defense + Veil is what lets it tank Iron Heads and set up.
4. **Baxcalibur closes.** Ice Shard the 4×-weak dragons (Garchomp), High Horsepower the Steels, and **Glaive Rush only as the final blow** (mind the double-damage drawback after).
5. **Gholdengo / Incineroar patch the rest** — Gholdengo breaks special walls and status-immunes; Incineroar Intimidates, Fake-Outs, and Rocky-Helmet-punishes the physical attackers.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **Launch-week team, by the creator's own admission.** "This is the very beginning of the regulation, so some things are subject to change." It's an early-meta exploration with a strong core, not a tournament-proven list. Moves/items/abilities are well-sourced (he narrated them all); **EV spreads/natures are reconstructions.**
- **Mega Golisopod is a real problem — it's what he lost to.** The one shown loss was to a **Mega Golisopod** (Bug/Steel, Tough Claws) — Baxcalibur has no reliable Fire coverage, and Golisopod's **Iron Head** "tough-claws-boosted STAB just chunks" even through Aurora Veil, while **Leech Life** heals it back. The creator flags that bringing **Incineroar** (Flare Blitz) instead might have helped, but the opponent would just switch in **Pelipper rain** to weaken the Fire. See [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-golisopod-sirfetchd-rain-team|the Mega Golisopod guide]].
- **The terrain is a timer.** Psychic Terrain lasts 5 turns (8 with a Terrain Extender, which isn't run here), and Indeedee is a **Focus-Sash / frail** piece — lose it and both the priority-shield *and* the Expanding-Force spam are gone. There's only one terrain setter.
- **Expanding Force spam has hard walls.** It does **nothing to Dark-types** (e.g. it "has no effect" on the opposing Incineroar/Gholdengo-adjacent picks the creator called out), and a **Mega Golisopod / bulky special-defense** pivot eats it. Have a plan B (Baxcalibur, Gholdengo) when the spam is walled.
- **Glaive Rush is a trap if mis-timed** — after it, Baxcalibur takes double damage and can't dodge until it moves again, so a follow-up KOs it. Use it to finish, not to open.
- **Verification note (this one held up).** Unlike the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-golisopod-sirfetchd-rain-team|Golisopod]] guide's cutoff-bound fleet, the adversarial pass here was **web-enabled and confirmed every Champions claim** (Mega Baxcalibur / Thermal Exchange / snow +50% Def / Psychic-Terrain priority-block / Expanding-Force spread / the Sneasler Seed→Unburden combo). The **only** correction folded in: **Make It Rain self-drops Sp. Atk by 2 (and is a spread move)** in Champions — reflected above. A completeness critic also flagged that **High Horsepower is 95 BP** (a couple of the topic's older Steelix/Gengar entries call it 85 — a real cross-wiki inconsistency worth a cleanup pass, left untouched here per "ask before editing existing notes").

## Key Takeaways

- **Psychic Terrain does two jobs, and the *defensive* one is the point.** It blanks all priority on both sides — which is exactly how a **Speed-87 Mega Baxcalibur** (too fast for Trick Room, too slow for the meta) survives what would normally revenge-kill it. The offense (both-slots **Expanding Force**) is the bonus.
- **Mega Baxcalibur is a snow-boosted brick.** **Thermal Exchange** makes Fire teams (Charizard-Y) *buff* it instead of burning it, **snow adds +50% Defense**, and Aurora Veil + Coaching stack on top — "you are a tank."
- **The Sneasler engine is real tech.** **Psychic Seed** in Indeedee's terrain = **+1 Sp. Def *and* an Unburden Speed-double** at once, making Sneasler the fastest thing on the field — the same Unburden payoff the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-banette-swagger-team|Mega Banette team]] reaches a completely different way (Swagger + Lum).
- **Snow + Aurora Veil is the durability layer** — the sibling of the topic's [[pokemon-champions-team-building/tailwind-snow-ho-team|Snow Hyper-Offense]] build, here bolted onto a tank instead of a fast core.
- **Honest launch-week caveat** — a strong, fun early-Reg-M-C core the creator himself says is provisional; its clean loss to **Mega Golisopod** marks the matchup to shore up.
