## Identity: a Steel knight that rain keeps alive, and a real knight that crits through everything

The advertised build-around is **Mega Golisopod**. The Champions Mega is the story: regular Golisopod is **Bug/Water**, but **on Mega Evolution it becomes Bug/Steel** and trades Emergency Exit for **Tough Claws** (contact moves ×1.3). That's a Scizor-shaped Pokémon — a monstrous **resistance table** (Bug/Steel resists ~10 types and is "very, very hard to remove," as the creator puts it), a base-45 Speed that loves **Trick Room**, and one glaring hole: a **4× weakness to Fire**. Rain is the answer, so the team is built on a **Pelipper Drizzle** core that halves Fire and keeps the knight standing.

But the title's twist is real: the pick that actually carries games is **Sirfetch'd**, "the best knight in the game." Holding its signature **Leek**, its high-crit **Leaf Blade** becomes a **guaranteed critical hit** — and crits **ignore the target's Defense/Sp.Def boosts and its screens**. That plus a **Meteor Assault** (buffed to a 170-BP "Giga Impact that can't miss") off ~205 Attack is a wall-breaker the meta doesn't respect until it's too late.

## The pieces (how the shell props up the two knights)

- **Mega Golisopod (Bug/Steel, Tough Claws).** The build-around. **First Impression** is a **+2-priority, contact Bug move buffed 90→100 BP** in Champions — Tough Claws pushes it to ~130 effective, and it's the creator's favourite (over Swords Dance) because it hits *now*. **Iron Head** is the Tough-Claws-boosted Steel STAB (with flinch), and **Leech Life** (80 BP, heals 50%) is the sustain — "the way he survives." Base ability Emergency Exit would yank it out at half HP, but the **Mega ability is Tough Claws**, so it stays in and keeps swinging. Base 45 Speed → it wants to be **slow under Trick Room**.
- **Sirfetch'd (Scrappy + Leek) — the true knight.** **Leek** raises its crit ratio by **+2** (a 50% crit on its own); **Leaf Blade** is a high-crit move (+1); **+3 = a guaranteed critical hit**, exactly like the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/ash-xyz-anime-team|Ash XY&Z team]]'s Scope-Lens Goodra — and those crits **ignore the foe's Defense boosts and Reflect/Light Screen**. **Scrappy** does double duty: it makes Sirfetch'd **immune to Intimidate** (so it fights through Incineroar) *and* lets **Close Combat hit Ghosts**. **Meteor Assault** is now the **highest-BP Fighting move in the game (170)**, 100% accurate — a "juiced Giga Impact that can't miss," at the cost of a recharge turn.
- **Pelipper (Drizzle).** The load-bearing partner. Rain **halves Fire damage**, directly answering Golisopod's 4× Fire weakness, and it powers **Weather Ball** (becomes Water, 100 BP) and **Hurricane** (never-miss in rain). The creator runs **Wide Guard** + a surprise **Ice Beam** over Protect/Tailwind — Wide Guard blanks the format's spread spam (**Dazzling Gleam, Hyper Voice, Expanding Force, Eruption, Heat Wave**), and **Ice Beam is a 4× surprise on Mega Salamence**. **Sitrus Berry** for bulk. (**Politoed** is the listed alternative Drizzle setter.)
- **Farigiraf (Trick Room).** The **only** Trick Room setter, and it's chosen for durability — it's "pretty hard to take out," and **Armor Tail blocks opposing priority**, so the setter can't be Fake-Out-flinched off its Trick Room. He swapped his usual **Colbur Berry** (anti-Dark) for a **Tanga Berry** (anti-Bug) specifically because the meta is full of bug/Golisopod mirrors. **Helping Hand + Protect** round it out, with a flex attack (he sometimes runs Thunderbolt).
- **Incineroar.** The glue and the **Intimidate**. Run **Adamant with no Speed** (a Trick-Room-friendly spread), it brings **Fake Out / Parting Shot** tempo and pivots the rain and Intimidate around. (He nicknamed it "Wolfe, King of VGC.")
- **Primarina (Life Orb, Calm Mind).** The **Mega Salamence answer**: bulky **Calm Mind** setup + **Moonblast** (Fairy, 2× on Salamence). Quiet (min-Speed for Trick Room). On a rain team, **Liquid Voice** (turns Hyper Voice into a rain-boosted Water spread move) is the stronger competitive ability — but its Salamence-killing job is Moonblast either way.

## Game plan

1. **Rain first, then decide on Trick Room.** Lead **Pelipper** to lock in rain (Fire-proofing Golisopod and arming Weather Ball). Bring **Farigiraf** for Trick Room when you need the slow knights to move first — but note the creator says his team is "leagues better *outside* of Trick Room" against some boards, so treat TR as a tool, not a mandate.
2. **Wide Guard blanks the spread meta.** Against the Expanding-Force / Hyper-Voice / Eruption / Heat-Wave teams that spam the field, Pelipper's Wide Guard buys free turns while Golisopod/Sirfetch'd set up damage.
3. **Sirfetch'd breaks walls.** Click **Leaf Blade** into anything that's boosted its defenses or hidden behind screens (the crit ignores both); **Meteor Assault** to delete something outright (then respect the recharge); **Close Combat** even into Ghosts (Scrappy).
4. **Golisopod cleans under rain.** First Impression for immediate priority pressure, Iron Head for the flinch/Steel STAB, Leech Life to stay healthy — it's "very hard to remove" once rain neutralizes Fire.
5. **Primarina handles Mega Salamence** (Moonblast), and **Pelipper's Ice Beam** is the 4× backup if it tries to set up.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **It's a ladder showcase with mixed results.** The creator went up and down the ladder and **lost several shown games** — and he's candid that **most losses were his own misplays** ("I deserve to lose, I threw this so hard") plus some brutal RNG (enemy crits landing on his Sirfetch'd at the worst moments — ironic for a team that *forces* its own crits). The sets below are **reconstructions from his commentary**, not a transcribed paste.
- **Golisopod's 4× Fire weakness is real** — that's the price of the Bug/Steel Mega typing (the creator is right about it). If rain is gone or the foe pairs strong Fire with weather control, **Mega Golisopod evaporates**. Pelipper is not optional.
- **Sirfetch'd's guaranteed crit is Leaf-Blade-only.** Leek+Leaf-Blade is 100%, but **Meteor Assault and Close Combat crit at normal rates.** And Sirfetch'd hates **burn** (it ate multiple Will-O-Wisps on stream) and **recharge turns** after Meteor Assault.
- **One Trick Room setter.** Lose Farigiraf early and the slow-mode plan is gone — lean on the "better outside Trick Room" line and pivot to rain offense.
- **Verifier false-refutations, overturned (5).** Per the vault's wiki-verify rule, the adversarial Haiku fleet **REFUTED five *correct* claims** — "Golisopod can't Mega Evolve / no such Mega exists," "Leek is only +1, so ~25% crit," "Scrappy doesn't block Intimidate," "Pelipper doesn't have Drizzle (that's Kyogre's)," and "Leech Life is 75 BP." **All five are cutoff artifacts** (the fleet's knowledge predates Champions) and are independently overturned: Serebii confirms **Mega Golisopod = Bug/Steel + Tough Claws** and **First Impression 90→100 / Meteor Assault 150→170**; Bulbapedia/Smogon confirm **Leek+Leaf-Blade = guaranteed crit** and **Scrappy blocks Intimidate (Gen 8+)**; **Leech Life is 80 BP**; and **PokéSynergy itself resolved Pelipper's Drizzle, Sirfetch'd's Scrappy, and the Golisopod species cleanly**. The **one** legitimate fold-in: **Hyper Voice is Normal-type**, so it does *neutral* damage to Salamence — **Moonblast** (Fairy) is the actual answer.

## Key Takeaways

- **The Champions Mega changes everything about Golisopod** — Bug/Water → **Bug/Steel + Tough Claws**, giving it a Scizor-like resistance wall and a **First Impression** (buffed 90→100, ×1.3 contact) it hits like a truck with. The catch is a **4× Fire weakness**, which is *why* the whole team runs on **Pelipper Drizzle** rain.
- **The real star is Sirfetch'd** — **Leek + high-crit Leaf Blade = a guaranteed critical hit** that **ignores the foe's Defense boosts and screens**, and **Scrappy** lets it shrug off Intimidate *and* Close Combat Ghosts. **Meteor Assault (170 BP)** is the biggest Fighting move in the game. The topic's **second item-based guaranteed-crit engine**, next to the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/ash-xyz-anime-team|Ash XY&Z]] Goodra.
- **Rain + Trick Room, flexibly** — Pelipper (rain, Wide Guard, Ice Beam) + Farigiraf (Trick Room, Armor-Tail-protected) form a slow-mode shell, but the creator plays it as a tool ("leagues better outside Trick Room" sometimes), not a lock.
- **Honest showcase** — a semi-meta Golisopod-rain core (many people run Golisopod post-Tough-Claws) plus a **spice pick** that overperformed; mixed ladder results the creator pins on his own misplays and RNG. Sets reconstructed.
- **Verify the verifier** — the skeptic fleet threw **five false refutations** on correct Champions mechanics; all overturned with sources + the tool's own clean resolution. The one real correction: **Hyper Voice is Normal**, so **Moonblast** is the Salamence answer.
