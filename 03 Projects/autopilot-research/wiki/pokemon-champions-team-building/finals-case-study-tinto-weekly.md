## The bracket, briefly

A ~3-hour finals-day stream ran the top cut (quarterfinals → semifinals → grand final). The grand final came down to **Lương Thạnh** — a player who represented Vietnam at the VR World Cup, praised for "textbook / scientific" play, who reached the final **2–0** on skill — versus **Jank Ken**, a multi-time winner of the weekly cup piloting a well-known meta build. **Jank Ken won the final 2–0** to take the title.

| Mon | Shown doing |

## How the final played out

**Game 1 — the Kingambit clinic.** Jank Ken used **Sneasler Fake Out + double-Protect reads** to buy **Kingambit a free Swords Dance**, and from there it snowballed — a +2 **Sucker Punch** / Iron Head that the casters said "no one can stop, like a god." Lương Thạnh played tightly (a great Sucker-Punch predict of his own) and even got slightly unlucky (an **Earth Power roll came low**, leaving Sneasler at 2% instead of KO'd), but couldn't break the set-up Kingambit. **Kingambit was named the game's MVP. Jank Ken 1–0.**

**Game 2 — winning the weather war.** Lương Thạnh added **Sylveon** and pushed aggressively with his sun, but Jank Ken brought **Mega Froslass**: **Snow Warning overwrote the sun**, turning off Venusaur's Chlorophyll and powering up a 100%-accurate **Blizzard** that's **4× on Garchomp**. With the sun team's speed control and its Garchomp neutralized, **Lycanroc's priority Accelerock** cleaned up. The casters called it — Jank Ken "crushed Lương Thạnh, almost unable to breathe." **Jank Ken 2–0, champion.**

## Lessons (why it's a case study)

- **Set up *once*, then win.** The whole game 1 plan was to protect a single **Swords Dance Kingambit** turn (Fake Out + double-Protect), because a boosted Kingambit with priority Sucker Punch is nearly unstoppable. Enabling one snowball threat > spreading damage — the same lesson as the [Tín Tồ VGC Cup final](finals-case-study-tinto-cup.html) (protect the win-con).
- **Reads decide technical finals.** The casters stressed there was *almost no luck* — it was a prediction battle (double-Protect vs Fake Out, Sucker-Punch timing). The one luck swing (a low Earth Power roll) went *against* the loser and still didn't matter.
- **Weather wars counter sun.** Jank Ken's **Mega Froslass Snow Warning** is the textbook answer to a **Charizard + Venusaur sun** team — snow overwrites the sun (killing Chlorophyll), and Blizzard 4×-ices the Garchomp. (The same Mega Froslass snow engine powers the topic's [Tailwind Snow-HO team](tailwind-snow-ho-team.html).)
- **Meta familiarity is an edge.** The casters noted Jank Ken's team is a **known, heavily-piloted meta build** (associated with the player "Zoria"), so he had replay data to prepare — while Lương Thạnh's line was less documented.

## Watch-out (honest note)

This is **match footage**: partial, unofficial teams (the sixth Pokémon of each side never appeared; a Scovillain sixth for Jank Ken was a caster's *guess*), and "sets" are only the moves seen. Treat it as a **piloting/decision study**, not a copy-paste team.

## Key Takeaways

- **Jank Ken won 2–0** by (game 1) protecting a **Swords Dance Kingambit** into an unstoppable snowball, and (game 2) **winning the weather war** with **Mega Froslass** snow over Lương Thạnh's Charizard/Venusaur sun + a **Lycanroc Accelerock** finish.
- **Protect the win-con + read the opponent** — the recurring theme of the topic's finals studies; luck barely featured.
- **Snow overwrites sun** (kills Chlorophyll, enables 100% Blizzard) — a clean weather-war counter, and Blizzard is 4× on Garchomp.
