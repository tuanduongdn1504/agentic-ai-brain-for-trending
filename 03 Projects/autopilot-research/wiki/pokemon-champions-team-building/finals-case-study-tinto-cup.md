# Finals case study — Tín Tồ VGC Cup: Heoway (champion) vs Jabarkas

> **Source:** [Jabarkas vs Heoway — chung kết Tín Tồ VGC cup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZMAyNXN_AYk) — `ZMAyNXN_AYk`, **Tín Tồ** (Vietnamese), 2026-08-23, **9:53**. A casted **grand-final** of a Vietnamese community cup.
> **This is a *match*, not a team build.** It reveals two *partial* teams through the play-by-play (there's no team-preview screen), so this is a **case study** — the Pokémon shown on-camera, the pivotal plays, and the result — **not** a full importable teamlist (I won't invent the hidden slots). Mechanics adversarially fact-checked.
> **The topic's second finals case study** (after [Steelix sand-vs-sun at Indianapolis](finals-case-study-sand-vs-sun.html)) — a study in *piloting*, not team-building.

## The matchup

**Heoway** (the eventual champion) ran a **Mega Floette bulky-offense** team; **Jabarkas** (runner-up; a first-time VGC entrant, the "Sushibot" restaurant owner) ran a **Sand + Trick Room** team. Both brought a **Sneasler**. Heoway had reached the final by beating "Zoria," a top-1 Vietnamese-server player.

### The two teams (as revealed on-camera — partial)

**Heoway — champion (bulky offense around Mega Floette)**

| Mon | Shown doing |
|---|---|
| **Mega Floette** | the protected win-con — Calm Mind + Mega, then Draining Kiss / **Moonblast** (Moonblast KO'd Annihilape; Fairy is SE on Fighting) |
| **Sneasler** | Fake Out + Close Combat pressure |
| **Incineroar** | Intimidate + Fake Out + Parting Shot — bodyguard/pivot |

**Jabarkas — runner-up (Sand + Trick Room)**

| Mon | Shown doing |

## How it played out

**Game 1 — Jabarkas pressures, Heoway protects.** Jabarkas led **Sneasler + Focus-Sash Excadrill** — Excadrill immediately threatens Floette (Iron Head) and Sneasler (Earthquake). Heoway pulled Floette and brought **Incineroar** (Intimidate) to blunt the pressure. Jabarkas's key play: **Coaching** from Sneasler onto Excadrill — undoing the Intimidate and adding +1 Defense — then **Mega Tyranitar** set sand for Sand Rush. Excadrill's **Earthquake KO'd two** at once. But Heoway had shielded **Mega Floette** behind **Kingambit's Sucker Punch** (a faster Excadrill can't safely attack into it), Floette Calm-Mind'd + Mega'd, and — the shock of the game — a boosted **Floette OHKO'd the Mega Tyranitar in its own sand**. Jabarkas's last was **Annihilape**, and Floette's **Moonblast** ended it. **Heoway 1–0.**

**The pivotal decision.** The caster called it out directly: Jabarkas should have brought **Sinistcha instead of Annihilape** in game 1. Sinistcha's **Rage Powder** would have redirected/neutralized Kingambit's **Sucker Punch**, freeing Excadrill to Earthquake the board unimpeded. Bringing Annihilape (which had no answer to the Sucker Punch bodyguard) is what let Floette set up and take over — a **team-preview bring error**, not a play error.

**Game 2 — Trick Room, but the win-con holds again.** Jabarkas went **Sinistcha Trick Room** (slow Sneasler moves first) + Rage Powder + Dire Claw, and even got a lucky **Dire Claw sleep** on… his read went sideways when his own Tyranitar got slept. Heoway's **Dragonite (Multiscale)** came down; Sinistcha's **Matcha Gotcha** chipped it to break Multiscale, but **Annihilape's Rage Fist couldn't close**, and Heoway's Sneasler + Floette + Dragonite ground it out. **Heoway takes the set.**

## Lessons (why this earns a case-study slot)

- **Protecting the win-con beats raw pressure.** Jabarkas had the stronger *board* at points (Coaching Excadrill + sand), but Heoway's **Kingambit/Incineroar bodyguards kept Mega Floette alive to set up** — and a set-up Floette simply took over. Shielding one snowball threat > spreading pressure.
- **Games are decided at team preview.** The single most important moment was a **bring decision** (Annihilape over Sinistcha), not an in-game misclick. Against a Sucker-Punch bodyguard, the redirection piece (Sinistcha Rage Powder) was the correct fourth — and leaving it in the box cost the game.
- **Mega Floette is a top-meta monster.** It **OHKO'd a Mega Tyranitar inside sandstorm** after a Calm Mind — the same Pokémon anchoring the topic's [#1 ladder team](floette-coaching-dual-mega-team.html), the [double-Trick-Room team](floette-double-trickroom-team.html), and the winning [Indianapolis sun team](finals-case-study-sand-vs-sun.html).
- **Multiscale is chip-vulnerable.** Dragonite's Multiscale evaporates the moment it takes any damage — a spread move like **Matcha Gotcha** "breaks" it for a teammate to follow up.

## Key Takeaways

- **Heoway won by protecting Mega Floette** (Kingambit Sucker Punch + Incineroar Intimidate as bodyguards) and letting it Calm-Mind sweep — even OHKOing a Mega Tyranitar in sand.
- **Jabarkas lost at team preview** — bringing Annihilape over Sinistcha meant no answer to the Sucker-Punch wall protecting Floette.
- **Coaching Sneasler + Sand (Tyranitar/Excadrill)** is strong pressure, but pressure alone didn't crack a well-shielded win-con.
