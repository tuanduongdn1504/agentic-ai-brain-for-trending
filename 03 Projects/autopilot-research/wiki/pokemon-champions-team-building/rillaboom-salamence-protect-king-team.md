## Identity: everything exists to make Kingambit win

Duy's design brief was explicit: **double-Intimidate cores (Incineroar + Salamence) and Indeedee are dominating day one, and Kingambit beats all of them** — Sucker Punch / Kowtow Cleave punish Indeedee and psychic-spam, and Kingambit doesn't care about Intimidate. So he built a **"protect the King" support shell** around a Swords-Dance Kingambit, using the two new Reg-M-C toys as the glue:

- **Rillaboom** keeps Kingambit healthy: **Grassy Terrain heals 1/16 per turn** and, crucially, **halves Earthquake** — one of the few things that threatens a Dark/Steel Kingambit — while **Fake Out** buys it a free Swords Dance and **Grassy Glide** adds priority.
- **Mega Salamence** brings **Intimidate on entry** (softening physical threats for the King) and then Mega-evolves into an **Aerilate Double-Edge** nuke with **Tailwind** for team speed.

It's a **dual-Mega hyper-offense** — you carry both **Mega Salamence and Mega Floette** and pick which to Mega per matchup — and a flexible one: Duy stresses you choose a key attacker plus a Fake-Out partner for each opponent.

## The pieces (how the shell props up the King)

- **Kingambit (Supreme Overlord).** The win-con. **Supreme Overlord** adds ~10% Attack per fainted ally (up to +50%), so on a hyper-offense that trades, a late **Swords-Dance Kingambit** hits absurdly hard. **Kowtow Cleave never misses**, **Sucker Punch** is Dark priority that punishes the Indeedee / psychic / setup leads the meta is full of, and it shrugs off Intimidate. Duy invests a little Speed to win the Kingambit mirror.
- **Rillaboom (Grassy Surge).** The King's bodyguard. **Grassy Terrain heals 1/16 per turn** (keeping Kingambit topped up) and **halves Earthquake/Bulldoze/Magnitude** (Kingambit is 2× weak to Ground) — a genuine defensive layer, not just chip-heal. **Fake Out** frees a Swords Dance turn; **Grassy Glide** is priority; **Wood Hammer / High Horsepower** are the damage.
- **Mega Salamence (Intimidate → Aerilate).** The other new toy. It enters with **Intimidate** (a free Attack drop protecting the team) and Megas into **Aerilate** — turning **Double-Edge into a ~144-BP Flying-type nuke** that, as Duy puts it, "drains a target's whole health bar in one move." **Tailwind** is the team's speed control, and **Rock Slide** is his added tech to finish the **Charizards** the build otherwise struggles to close.
- **Mega Floette (Fairy Aura).** The second Mega and an alternate carry — **Fairy Aura boosts every Fairy move +33%**, so a **Calm-Mind Dazzling Gleam** (spread) chunks both foes while **Draining Kiss** (heals 75% of damage) keeps it alive. This is the bulky World-Champ setup build; Duy notes it may want re-tuning for the new format (PokéSynergy shows **Moonblast / Light of Ruin** as higher-usage alternatives to Draining Kiss).
- **Sneasler (Unburden, Grassy Seed) — the 4th way to trigger Unburden.** In Rillaboom's Grassy Terrain the **Grassy Seed** is consumed for **+1 Defense**, and losing the item procs **Unburden → double Speed**. Now the fastest thing on the field, it brings **Fake Out**, **Close Combat / Dire Claw** offense, and — the key support move — **Coaching** to hand Kingambit or Salamence **+1 Atk / +1 Def** (Coaching even passes *through* an ally's Protect). The topic reaches this same Unburden payoff three other ways: **Psychic Seed** ([[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-baxcalibur-terrain-team|Chill's Baxcalibur]]), **White Herb** ([[pokemon-champions-team-building/baxcalibur-grassy-imprison-team|Braeden's Baxcalibur]]), and **Swagger + Lum** ([[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-banette-swagger-team|Mega Banette]]).
- **Basculegion (Adaptability).** The flex sixth — **Adaptability** doubles its STAB, **Last Respects** (Ghost, +50 BP per fainted ally) snowballs late, **Aqua Jet** is priority, and it's Duy's answer to **Charizard** and **sand** teams. A Berry gives it enough bulk to tank a Kingambit Sucker Punch.

## Game plan

1. **Pick a key attacker + a Fake-Out partner per matchup.** Common pairs: **Kingambit + Sneasler** (Fake Out → Swords Dance), or **Mega Floette + Sneasler/Rillaboom**. Decide which Mega (Salamence vs Floette) fits the opponent before you lead.
2. **Set Grassy Terrain early** so Kingambit is healing and Earthquakes are halved, then Fake-Out it a free Swords Dance.
3. **Coach the King (or Salamence).** Sneasler procs Unburden off the Grassy Seed, then Coaches your win-con to +1/+1 — even through a Protect.
4. **Aerilate Double-Edge / Dazzling Gleam** for immediate spread pressure; **Tailwind** if you need the speed; **Sucker Punch / Kowtow Cleave** close.
5. **Bring Basculegion** into Charizard / sand; **Rock Slide (Salamence)** is the dedicated Charizard-finisher.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **Launch-week ladder team — the creator's own framing.** "Still early-season games, don't overthink it, we're testing." A flexible, strong HO core, not a proven list. Moves/items/abilities are well-sourced; **EVs/natures are reconstructions.**
- **Mega Golisopod is the shown loss — and an anecdotal pattern worth flagging (not a law).** Duy's one loss was to a **Mega Golisopod** team, and he pins it on having **no Fire coverage** (he considered Flamethrower on Salamence but didn't run it). Across the three launch-week showcases documented in this topic so far — [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-baxcalibur-terrain-team|Chill]], [[pokemon-champions-team-building/baxcalibur-grassy-imprison-team|Braeden]], and this one — *all three lost their shown game to a Mega Golisopod team*, and none carried a Fire move. The mechanical reason is solid (Mega Golisopod is Bug/Steel, **4× weak to Fire**); the "it's *the* defining launch-week problem" generalization is **anecdotal at N=3**, so treat it as a strong hint to pack Fire coverage, not a proven metagame law.
- **The fix is known.** Duy points to a **CyberVGC** player who went **10–2** in a large tournament with a near-identical team that swaps **Mega Floette → Hisuian Arcanine** — the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/hisuian-arcanine-speed-flex-team|Rock-Head Head-Smash Arcanine]] gives exactly the Fire answer to Golisopod this build lacks.
- **It's Frost-weak (Froslass) and it's a piloting-heavy team.** Salamence, Rillaboom, and Sneasler are all soft to **Mega Froslass** Blizzard, and Duy stresses you must study the matchup and pick the right four each game — a mis-pick (e.g., forgetting to check the enemy team) cost him tempo on stream.
- **Double-Edge has recoil** and Wave Crash/Close Combat self-chip — this HO trades HP for pressure, which *feeds* Supreme Overlord but can leave the King exposed if the trades go wrong.

## Key Takeaways

- **Build the shell, protect the King.** A Swords-Dance **Supreme-Overlord Kingambit** is the win-con; **Rillaboom** (Grassy heal + halve-Earthquake + Fake Out) and **Mega Salamence** (Intimidate + Aerilate + Tailwind) exist to keep it alive and set it up. Kingambit's Sucker Punch / Kowtow beat the day-one Indeedee / Intimidate / psychic cores.
- **Two new toys, two real payoffs** — **Aerilate Double-Edge** (a ~144-BP Flying nuke off Mega Salamence) and **Grassy Terrain** (heal + Ground-damage halving) are the reasons this shell works.
- **The topic's 4th Unburden-Sneasler trigger** — **Grassy Seed** here, joining Psychic Seed, White Herb, and Swagger+Lum. Same doubled-Speed sweeper, a fourth key.
- **Dual-Mega flexibility** — carry Salamence *and* Floette, pick per matchup; it's a versatile HO you must pilot deliberately.
- **Pack Fire coverage for Golisopod.** The honest, recurring hole across the launch-week teams — the CyberVGC fix is a **Hisuian Arcanine** in the Floette slot.
