## Identity: an old core made new, built around flexibility

The heart is the classic trio — **Mega Kangaskhan + Conkeldurr + Kingambit** (the last of these was **Bisharp** eleven years ago). Around it, Gonkas built a **dual-Mega, do-anything** team: **Charizard** is the second Mega, **Farigiraf** offers a Trick Room mode, and **Kommo-o** is dedicated anti-sun tech. Nothing here is locked in — you pick your Mega, your mode, and your leads per opponent.

## The dual-Mega mind game (Kangaskhan ↔ Charizard)

Both Megas keep **full value in base form**, which is what makes the flex so strong:
- **Base Kangaskhan (Scrappy):** Fake Out that **hits Ghosts** and ignores Intimidate — it can Fake-Out-lock a Basculegion or Gengar the rest of the team can't touch, and it shuts down Incineroar's Intimidate cycling. **Ice Punch** is the surprise that OHKOs Garchomp.
- **Mega Kangaskhan (Parental Bond):** every hit lands **twice** — Double-Edge / Sucker Punch become huge, and it's bulkier and faster.
- **Base Charizard (Solar Power):** +50% Special Attack (at 1/8 HP a turn) — a Heat Wave/Weather Ball nuke that's bulky enough to survive a Life-Orb Jolly Garchomp Rock Slide.
- **Mega Charizard-Y (Drought):** sets the sun the anti-Sun plan wants, and powers up Weather Ball (Fire, 100 BP in sun).

The bluff: opponents assume Charizard *must* be the Mega and treat Kangaskhan as a throwaway — so you Mega the **Kangaskhan** and Charizard *still* nukes with Solar Power. You decide which to Mega based on what they brought.

## The trio + support

- **Conkeldurr — the Steel-breaker (buffed).** Kangaskhan and Kingambit both **can't hit Steel types**; Conkeldurr one-shots them with **Close Combat** + **Mach Punch** priority. **Sheer Force + Life Orb** (Sheer Force removes the Life Orb recoil on its boosted moves) makes it hit absurdly hard. **Thunder Punch** is the spice: 4× on Pelipper, and real damage into Charizard/Basculegion — Gonkas's #1 answer to rain/Archaludon. Its catch: **thin Special Defense** — it gets blown up if you don't pivot it in safely behind Kangaskhan.
- **Kingambit — the modernized "Bisharp."** **Kowtow Cleave** (never-misses Dark STAB) + Sucker Punch priority + Iron Head, and the tech is **Occa Berry** — it survives a Charizard **Weather Ball in sun** so it can stand in front of a sun team, Kowtow a Basculegion, then Sucker Punch the Charizard. Great **anti-Delphox** lead alongside Farigiraf (Armor Tail blocks the Fake Out, immediate Kowtow pressure).
- **Farigiraf — Trick Room + weather answer.** **Armor Tail** shuts off enemy priority while it sets **Trick Room** (the team's slow-mode option vs fast teams); the **Thunderbolt** tech lets even a bulky, no-SpA Farigiraf OHKO Pelipper (~60–75%+) — locking in permanent sun weather with Charizard — and chip Basculegion. Bulky enough (Sitrus) to eat a Weather Ball.
- **Kommo-o — anti-Sun tech.** **Overcoat** makes it **immune to Sleep Powder / Spore**, so it walls the Venusaur/Vileplume sun leads that otherwise put your team to sleep; it's bulky enough to survive a Garchomp Dragon Claw + a Charizard double-up, then pressures with **Clanging Scales** (or sets up with **Clangorous Soul**).

## Game plan: freestyle by matchup

The team is deliberately **flexible** — Gonkas stresses you should *not* run the same leads every game:
- **vs Sun teams:** bring **Kangaskhan + Farigiraf + Kommo-o/Charizard** — Kommo-o ignores the sleep-powder leads, Kangaskhan Fake-Out-locks, Farigiraf Thunderbolts the Pelipper for permanent weather.
- **vs Rain / Archaludon:** bring **Conkeldurr** (Close Combat + Thunder Punch) — his go-to rain answer.
- **vs Delphox "defend-the-king" teams:** **Farigiraf + Kingambit** lead (no Fake Out gets through Armor Tail; immediate Kowtow Cleave).
- **vs fast teams:** flip to the **Farigiraf Trick Room** mode.
- Decide **which Mega** (Kangaskhan or Charizard) after seeing their picks; the other stays a fully-functional base-form Pokémon.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **Conkeldurr's Special Defense is a liability** — it can be OHKO'd from the special side, so it must pivot in safely (behind a Kangaskhan Fake Out), not lead raw.
- **It demands piloting.** The versatility is the strength *and* the cost — lots of decisions (which Mega, which mode, which four), so it rewards a player who reads the matchup, not autopilot.
- **Steel weakness on the core** — Kangaskhan (even Scrappy) and Kingambit can't dent Steels; the whole plan leans on Conkeldurr being available for that job.

## Key Takeaways

- **A 2014 core, modernized:** Mega Kangaskhan + Conkeldurr + **Kingambit** (was Bisharp) — Conkeldurr's **Close Combat** and Kingambit's evolution are what pushed it back into relevance.
- **Dual-Mega with zero downside:** Kangaskhan and Charizard both stay useful in base form (Scrappy Fake-Out / Solar Power nuke), so you Mega whichever the matchup wants — and bluff the one they expect.
- **Conkeldurr is the Steel-answer** the Kangaskhan/Kingambit core needs (Close Combat/Mach Punch), with Thunder Punch tech for Pelipper/rain — but pivot it in safely (frail on the special side).
- **Two speed/utility modes:** Farigiraf **Trick Room** for fast teams, and its **Thunderbolt** for permanent-weather control; **Kommo-o Overcoat** hard-walls sun sleep leads.
- **It's a versatility team** — powerful in a pilot who reads matchups, less so on autopilot.
