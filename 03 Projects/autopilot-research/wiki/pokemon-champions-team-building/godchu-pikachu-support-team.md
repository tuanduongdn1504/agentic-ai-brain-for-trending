## Identity: give the frail Pikachu a stage

The whole team exists to let a **physical Light Ball Pikachu** hit hard and survive. **Light Ball doubles Pikachu's Attack** (its 107 in-game Attack becomes ~214), so a max-Attack Jolly Pikachu clicking **Volt Tackle (120 BP)** does real damage — but with **60/70 defenses**, it dies to a stiff breeze. So the other five are a **support shell**: double **Intimidate**, dual **screens**, a **Rage Powder** redirector with **healing**, and a special attacker to cover what the physical stars can't.

The neat structural pairing is **Pikachu + Staraptor**: Staraptor is **Flying** (immune to the Ground moves Pikachu fears) with **Intimidate** to blunt physical threats like Garchomp, while **Pikachu's Lightning Rod** soaks the Electric attacks Staraptor fears. Each covers the other's weakness.

## The pieces (how the shell props up Godchu)

- **Pikachu (Godchu).** Full physical **AD** with **Light Ball** doubling Attack → a hard-hitting **Volt Tackle**, plus **Fake Out** for tempo. **Lightning Rod** draws in Electric to shield the team. It's the payoff, but it's paper-thin — everything else is there to keep it alive.
- **Staraptor.** The co-star physical attacker and Pikachu's natural partner (Ground-immune + Intimidate ↔ Lightning-Rod protection). **Brave Bird / Close Combat** hit hard; note even a **Kingambit** struggles to touch it (no good move into a Flying attacker). *Regular Staraptor with Intimidate — not the Mega.*
- **Grimmsnarl.** **Prankster** priority **Reflect + Light Screen** are what actually let a 60/70-defense Pikachu live a hit; **Parting Shot** pivots and chips the foe's offense.
- **Gholdengo.** The special-coverage answer to the **Psychic/Fairy** types that wall Staraptor. **Good as Gold** shuts off enemy status; **Choice Specs Make It Rain** nukes (though it self-drops SpA and the format nerfed it to ~95% acc + −2 SpA, so fire it and reposition), with Shadow Ball / Thunderbolt / Power Gem coverage.
- **Sinistcha.** The healer/redirector: **Hospitality** heals an ally 25% on entry, **Matcha Gotcha** is a self-healing Grass nuke, **Life Dew** tops up the team, and **Rage Powder** pulls single-target attacks off the frail Pikachu. **Kasib Berry** softens a super-effective Ghost hit.
- **Incineroar.** Standard glue and the **second Intimidate** — Fake Out + Parting Shot + Knock Off round out the support.

## Game plan

1. **Set the stage, then swing.** Get **screens up (Grimmsnarl)** and an **Intimidate** or two down, then bring Pikachu in behind **Rage Powder / Fake Out** so it can Volt Tackle safely.
2. **Lead the synergy pair** — Pikachu + Staraptor covers each other's weaknesses and applies immediate pressure; keep Sinistcha/Grimmsnarl in the back to enable.
3. **Pivot to re-Intimidate** — bounce Incineroar/Staraptor in and out with Parting Shot to keep grinding the opponent's Attack down while you heal with Sinistcha.
4. **Gholdengo handles the walls** — bring it for the Psychic/Fairy/Steel matchups the physical stars can't crack.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **It's a showcase, not a tournament team.** The creator lost the first shown game to a bad matchup and is candid that Pikachu is a meme-tier pick he's "paying off" for viewers. Treat the sets as **reconstructions** and the ceiling as "fun that sometimes wins."
- **Pikachu is extraordinarily frail** — practically any neutral hit KOs it, so it does *nothing* without screens + Intimidate + redirection first. If the shell is disrupted, Godchu contributes little.
- **Make It Rain self-nerfs** — it drops Gholdengo's Sp.Atk by 2 and sits at ~95% accuracy, so it's a one-and-reposition move, not spam.
- **Rage Powder is dodge-able** — Grass-types, Overcoat, and Safety Goggles ignore the redirect, exposing Pikachu.
- **Verifier false-refutation, overturned** (per the vault's wiki-verify rule): a checker claimed **"Intimidate doesn't stack — two Intimidate users still only give the opponent −1."** That's **wrong**: each Intimidate Pokémon lowers the foe's Attack by a stage *on entry*, and stat stages accumulate — so **Staraptor + Incineroar (plus pivoting) grind the opponent down past −1**, which is the entire point of a double-Intimidate core. (The checker also asserted "no Mega Staraptor exists" — also wrong for Champions per the topic's Serebii checks — but it's moot here, since this build uses the *regular* Intimidate Staraptor.)

## Key Takeaways

- **Build the shell, then the star shines** — a frail **Light Ball Pikachu** (doubled Attack, Volt Tackle) is carried by **double Intimidate + dual screens + Rage-Powder redirect + Hospitality/Life Dew healing**. The support is 80% of the team.
- **Pikachu + Staraptor is the synergy core** — Staraptor's Flying/Intimidate covers Pikachu's Ground weakness; Pikachu's Lightning Rod covers Staraptor's Electric weakness.
- **Gholdengo patches the walls** — Choice Specs coverage for the Psychic/Fairy/Steel that stop the physical attackers, with Good as Gold blocking status.
- **A fun/for-content build** — honest sibling to the topic's [Zoroark team](zoroark-illusion-fun-team.html) (same creator) and a cousin to the [Ash XY&Z team](ash-xyz-anime-team.html)'s Light Ball Pikachu — showcase energy, mixed results, reconstructed sets.
- **Two Intimidate users stack** (contra a bad skeptic call) — that repeated Attack-drop pressure, plus screens, is *why* a paper-thin Pikachu can function at all.
