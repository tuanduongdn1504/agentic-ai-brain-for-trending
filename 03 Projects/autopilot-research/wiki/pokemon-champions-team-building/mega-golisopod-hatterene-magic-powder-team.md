## Identity: turn the counter into the meal

Mega Golisopod's problem is famous: it's **Bug/Steel with a 4× Fire weakness**, so a Charizard or a Torkoal normally deletes it. BlindJon's team flips that with one move — **Hatterene's signature Magic Powder**, which **changes a target's type to pure Psychic**:

- **Offensively:** Magic Powder the **opposing** Fire attacker (a Charizard, say) → it's now **pure Psychic** → **Golisopod's Leech Life** (a Bug contact move, **2× super-effective vs Psychic**, boosted by **Tough Claws**, and it **drains 50%**) **one-shots it**. BlindJon eats *two* Charizards with Leech Life in one video — "reversing prey and predator."
- **Defensively (a niche escape):** Magic Powder your **own** Golisopod (a single-target move can hit your ally in doubles) → Golisopod becomes **pure Psychic**, which **erases the 4× Fire weakness** (Psychic takes Fire at neutral) and lets it tank a hit it otherwise couldn't. *The catch:* as a pure Psychic-type it **loses STAB on Leech Life and Iron Head** that turn, so this is a survival tool, not an offensive pivot — BlindJon lists it as an option, and mostly leans on the offensive use.

Around that trick sits a **Trick Room psychic-spam** core: **Hatterene + Indeedee-Female** both set Trick Room and stack **Psychic Terrain + Helping Hand** behind **Expanding Force** — which, as BlindJon puts it, just deletes any team that "doesn't have a dark type, a Wide Guard user, or a terrain setter." And because Trick Room isn't always right, the team has a **second gear**: **Pelipper Tailwind + a Psychic-Seed Unburden Sneasler** for a fast mode against faster opponents.

## The pieces

- **Mega Golisopod (Tough Claws).** The win condition and, thanks to Magic Powder, the Charizard-eater. **Bulk Up** turns its already-huge physical bulk into a wall that sweeps; **Leech Life** is the drain that heals it back to full (and 2× + Tough Claws into a Powdered foe); **Iron Head** is STAB; **Protect** buys Trick Room turns and dodges the Fire hits it fears. *Mega-evolve early:* its base ability **Emergency Exit** yanks it out when a hit drops it below 50% HP — you want **Tough Claws** (from Mega) so it stops fleeing mid-combo.
- **Hatterene (Magic Bounce, Life Orb).** The engine. **Magic Powder** is the whole gimmick; **Trick Room** sets the mode; **Expanding Force** (spread + boosted under its own Psychic Terrain) and **Dazzling Gleam** (spread Fairy) are the psychic-spam. **Magic Bounce** reflects Taunt/Will-O-Wisp/hazards. It's very slow — perfect under Trick Room.
- **Indeedee-Female (Psychic Surge, Mental Herb).** The support spine: **Psychic Surge** sets the terrain that boosts Expanding Force *and* blocks enemy priority (Fake Out, Sucker Punch, Grassy Glide — and even Prankster Taunt, which an opposing Whimsicott learns the hard way); **Follow Me** redirects a Weather Ball or Final Gambit away from Hatterene/Golisopod; **Helping Hand** super-charges an Expanding Force; **Trick Room** is a *second* setter so the mode survives losing Hatterene; **Mental Herb** blocks a Taunt/Encore that would stop the setup.
- **Pelipper (Drizzle, Focus Sash).** The flex support. **Drizzle** rain waters down enemy Fire (partly — see caveats), powers **Hurricane** (100% accuracy) and a Water **Weather Ball**; against enemy sun the Weather Ball flips to **Fire** (BlindJon chips a Charizard with it); **Wide Guard** blanks a spread attack to protect the frail psychics; **Tailwind** is the **speed-mode** enabler.
- **Sneasler (Unburden, Psychic Seed).** The speed-mode breaker. In Psychic Terrain the **Psychic Seed** gives +1 Sp. Def and is consumed → **Unburden doubles Speed**. *Note the tension:* doubled Speed is **anti-synergistic with Trick Room** — so Sneasler is the alternate-plan attacker (Close Combat / Dire Claw / Rock Slide) for games you play *fast*, not slow.
- **Basculegion-M (Adaptability, Choice Scarf).** The other speed-mode piece — a **Choice-Scarf Adaptability** cleaner (Last Respects / Wave Crash / Aqua Jet / Flip Turn) that snowballs late once allies have fainted. A flex you bring when Trick Room isn't the plan.

## Game plan

1. **Pick your mode at team preview.** Slow, bulky, or Fire-heavy enemy → **Trick Room mode** (Golisopod + Hatterene + Indeedee). Fast, frail enemy → **speed mode** (Pelipper Tailwind + Unburden Sneasler + Scarf Basculegion).
2. **Free psychic-spam vs the unprepared.** If the opponent has **no dark-type, no Wide Guard, and no terrain setter**, just lead **Hatterene + Indeedee** and click **Expanding Force** — it deletes the board.
3. **Set Trick Room, then Magic Powder.** Get TR up (redirect threats with Follow Me / Mental Herb protects the setup), Mega-evolve Golisopod, then **Magic Powder the Fire threat into Psychic** and **Leech Life** it for the OHKO — or Powder your **own** Golisopod to shrug off the Fire hit.
4. **Bulk Up and drain.** A +1/+1 Golisopod healing 50% per Leech Life is nearly impossible to break without a Fire move; Protect on the turns you expect one.
5. **Wide Guard the spread turns**, and use Pelipper's rain/Weather Ball to chip Fire attackers you can't Powder.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **Fire still ends Golisopod — even in rain.** The 4× weakness is the whole team's soft spot. BlindJon loses Golisopod to a **Torkoal Eruption in the rain** and to a **Mystical Fire Indeedee** on stream — "always Protect your Golisopod against Fire, even in rain," and Magic-Powder-your-own-Golisopod only helps if you have the turn to do it.
- **Magic Powder has holes.** It's a **powder move**, so it **fails vs Grass-types, Overcoat, and Safety Goggles**, and it's undone the moment the target **Terastallizes or changes form** — don't assume the Powder sticks against those. And Powdering your **own** Golisopod is **defensive-only** — pure-Psychic Golisopod loses STAB on Leech Life/Iron Head, so it's an escape hatch, not a way to keep hitting hard.
- **Trick Room and Unburden Sneasler are opposite plans.** This is a **dual-mode** team, not a synergy — a doubled-Speed Sneasler moves *last* under Trick Room. Commit to one mode per game; BlindJon protects a mistimed Sneasler rather than attack with it.
- **Emergency Exit can ruin a combo.** Before you Mega-evolve, a hit under 50% **switches Golisopod out** involuntarily — Mega early to lock in Tough Claws.
- **It's not invincible.** It went **4-1** across the shown games — the loss was a close Staraptor game swung by a **missed Rock Slide / a surprise Aerial Ace**. Ladder Bo1, small sample.

## Key Takeaways

- **Magic Powder is the whole team.** It changes a target to **pure Psychic**, so Golisopod's Tough-Claws **Leech Life hits 2× and drains 50%** to eat the very Fire attackers (Charizard) that normally 4×-OHKO it — and Powdering your *own* Golisopod erases its Fire weakness (defensively only — it loses STAB while pure Psychic). The counter becomes the meal.
- **Two Trick Room setters + Psychic Terrain + Expanding Force** = free wins vs any team lacking a **dark-type, Wide Guard, or terrain setter** — BlindJon's stated rule of thumb.
- **Dual-mode, not one plan:** Trick Room (slow Golisopod/Hatterene) OR a speed gear (Pelipper Tailwind + **Psychic-Seed Unburden Sneasler** + Scarf Basculegion). The Unburden Sneasler is the topic's **Psychic-Seed** trigger, reused here — but in the *fast* mode, deliberately at odds with the TR mode.
- **The 4× Fire weakness never fully goes away** — Magic-Powder-self and rain only partly cover it; Protect discipline vs Fire is mandatory (Golisopod still dies to Eruption in rain).
- **A distinct second Golisopod build** — where [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-golisopod-sirfetchd-rain-team|Zolrain's]] leaned on a guaranteed-crit Sirfetch'd engine, this one is a Magic-Powder Trick Room psychic-spam team that answers Golisopod's Fire problem head-on.
