## Identity: a dual-Mega sand balance team

The #2-most-popular team in Champions is **sand balance built on two Mega Stones**. **Tyranitar** sets sand and **Excadrill** cleans under it (Sand Rush = double Speed); **Mega Staraptor** brings Tailwind and a Contrary win-condition; **Milotic** and **Sinistcha** are the support backbone; **Gholdengo** is the special nuke. Carrying *both* Tyranitarite and Staraptite is the flex: the pilot decides each game whether to Mega the Tyranitar (commit to **Sand Stream**) or leave it and Mega the Staraptor (keep **Intimidate** pressure), and can hold the Tyranitar Mega back until a sun lead (Torkoal) shows so it can Mega in and overwrite the weather. (The honest cost of the flex: only one Mega fires per battle, so whichever stone you *don't* activate is a partly wasted item that game — the price the team pays for having both answers.)

## How it wins

- **Sand engine.** Tyranitar's Sand Stream turns on Excadrill's **Sand Rush** — a base-88 Excadrill hits ~176 effective Speed and cleans the board. Sand also chips non-Rock/Ground/Steel foes and pads Rock-types' Special Defense.
- **Two speed modes.** Either **Excadrill under sand** *or* **Mega Staraptor Tailwind** gives the team top-end Speed, so it wins the speed war against most builds.
- **Contrary win-con.** On Mega Evolution Staraptor becomes **Fighting/Flying** with **Contrary**, so its **Close Combat** flips the -Def/-SpD drops into **+Def/+SpD boosts** — it snowballs into a raid boss if you let it live.
- **Support glue.** Milotic (Scald burns, Icy Wind speed control, Haze reset) + Sinistcha (Rage Powder redirect, **Hospitality** heals an ally 25% on switch-in) keep the sweepers alive while Gholdengo loads Nasty Plot.

### The signature tech: Haze × Make It Rain

**Make It Rain lowers Gholdengo's own Special Attack (−1) each time it fires.** The team turns that drawback into a combo: **Milotic Hazes** (resetting all stat changes) to scrub Gholdengo's stacked debuff — the video shows Gholdengo sitting at **−2** — so it can immediately throw **another full-power Make It Rain**. Haze also wipes any Intimidate/Icy Wind drops the team has taken. (Caveat the pilot must respect: Haze removes their *own* boosts too.)

## How to beat it (IanKim's teardown, per Pokémon)

- **Mega Tyranitar** — **4× weak to Fighting**, so a 4× Close Combat / Bulk-Up sweeper ends it. But it's slow (61 / 71), so **speed-creep it** with a fast Fighting-type (Sneasler, Mega Staraptor, Mega Lopunny, Low-Kick Dragonite). Being a physical attacker, **Intimidate / Will-O-Wisp** slow it — **except Prankster status** (a Prankster Sableye's Will-O-Wisp fails: Dark-types are immune to Prankster-boosted status moves).
- **Excadrill** — kill the sand and you kill its Speed: **change the weather**, or out-speed it under your own **Tailwind**. Watch the **Focus Sash** (it survives one lethal hit).
- **Milotic** — its **Physical Defense (79) is the hole**; hit it physically. It's walled by Water/Electric (it only carries Scald + Icy Wind) — **Rotom-Wash, Mega Sceptile, Xurkitree, Meowscarada** all trouble it.
- **Sinistcha** — thin special bulk: **special super-effective hits** (e.g., Pelipper Hurricane) chunk it. Beat its **Rage Powder** two ways: **spread moves** (Heat Wave hits both targets *and* burns Sinistcha on the special side) or **Grass-types** (Grass ignores powder/Rage-Powder entirely and can fire freely at Sinistcha's ally).
- **Mega Staraptor** — before it Megas, **Defiant / Competitive punishes its Intimidate**. After it Megas it's **Contrary** (a different problem) — so **hit it hard *early*** before Close Combat stacks, and **punish the turn it clicks Tailwind** (it's open that turn).
- **Gholdengo** — **strong Ground moves** or **Sucker Punch** priority KO it. A mon that resists **both** its STABs (Make It Rain = Steel, Shadow Ball = Ghost) — **Incineroar** or **Kingambit** — walls it *and* hits back super-effectively (Dark → Ghost). **Wigglytuff** shuts it down (forces it onto Shadow Ball only). Good as Gold means **status *moves* don't land on it** — but Intimidate (an ability) still does.

## Team-level weaknesses

- **Accuracy variance.** The team leans on inaccurate moves (Make It Rain, Icy Wind, Rock Slide) — you can win games on a key miss. IanKim's honest tip: *"pray, pray hard."*
- **One special nuke.** Gholdengo is the *only* hard special attacker — **KO it early** and the team has no burst special damage left.
- **Two Intimidate-punishers.** Milotic and Mega Staraptor are what punish your Intimidate. Remove **both** and you can Intimidate the physical sweepers freely.
- **The double-Ghost bait.** Both Tyranitar's teammates *and* the team's Ghosts mean a reflexive **Fighting move clicked "into Tyranitar's slot" often whiffs** — Sinistcha/Gholdengo (Ghost) are immune to Fighting. Read before you click.

## Key Takeaways

- **It's dual-Mega sand balance** — Tyranitar (Sand Stream) + Excadrill (Sand Rush cleaner) + Mega Staraptor (Tailwind / Contrary), with Milotic + Sinistcha support and Gholdengo as the lone special nuke.
- **Two ways to break it:** deny the sand (kills Excadrill's Speed) and remove Gholdengo early (kills special pressure).
- **4× Fighting on Tyranitar is a trap** — the double-Ghost teammates make a mis-clicked Fighting move whiff; speed-creep and read first.
- **Grass-types and spread moves neutralise Sinistcha's Rage Powder** — the cleanest way around the redirect.
- **Contrary Mega Staraptor snowballs** — hit it *before* it stacks Close Combat.
