## Identity: same tank, a totally different shell

The star is the same as Chill's — **Mega Baxcalibur**, the Dragon/Ice brick with **Thermal Exchange** (Fire *buffs* it, can't be burned), Attack ~175, "base ~130 defense," almost-max-HP bulk. And the moveset is the launch-week consensus: **Ice Shard** (priority to snipe the 4×-weak Garchomp), **Glaive Rush** (the 120-BP Dragon finisher, mind the double-damage drawback after), **High Horsepower** (Steel coverage), **Protect**.

What's different is *everything around it*:

- **Grassy Terrain, not Psychic Terrain.** **Rillaboom's Grassy Surge** heals the bulky team 1/16 per turn ("the healing from terrain is so nice"), halves Earthquake, and hands Rillaboom a **priority Grassy Glide**.
- **An Imprison Farigiraf as dedicated anti-Trick-Room tech.** A Farigiraf that knows **both Imprison and Trick Room** can use **Imprison** to stop the opponent from using Trick Room at all — the creator's answer to the Mimikyu / Indeedee / Hatterene TR-and-Expanding-Force teams that give a Speed-87 Baxcalibur trouble. His own hard-learned lesson: *"If you see an Indeedee + Hatterene, no matter what, bring Farigiraf."*
- **A fast Hisuian Arcanine closer** (Rock Head Head Smash "like a Choice Band") instead of a snow setter.

## The pieces (how the Grassy shell works)

- **Mega Baxcalibur (Thermal Exchange).** Same tank, same job: tank hits, Ice Shard the dragons, Glaive Rush to finish, High Horsepower the Steels. Grassy Terrain heals it every turn and **halves Earthquake** (a common way to threaten a grounded Dragon/Ice). Thermal Exchange means Fire coverage *buffs* it. "Mega Bax is so freaking strong."
- **Rillaboom (Grassy Surge).** The terrain engine and a **Fake Out** lead. Grassy Terrain's chip-heal keeps the bulky core topped up, **Grassy Glide** is a priority Grass STAB, and the terrain **denies the opponent's own terrain** (only one terrain is active at a time). Bulky spread to pivot around.
- **Farigiraf (Imprison + Trick Room, Grassy Seed) — the tech piece.** This is the guide's headline. **Imprison** makes the opponent unable to use *any move Farigiraf also knows* — and because this Farigiraf **also knows Trick Room**, using Imprison **locks the enemy out of Trick Room entirely** (until Farigiraf faints or leaves). Against the Mimikyu / Indeedee / Hatterene TR-and-Expanding-Force teams that wall a slow Baxcalibur, that's a hard counter. **Twin Beam** (Farigiraf's signature Psychic 40-BP-×2) is its attack, **Grassy Seed** gives it +1 Defense in Rillaboom's terrain, and **Armor Tail** blocks opposing priority. The whole game 3 loss was, by his own account, "I should have brought Farigiraf" — the anti-TR piece he benched.
- **Sneasler (Unburden, White Herb) — the third way to trigger Unburden.** When **Close Combat** lowers Sneasler's Def/Sp.Def, the **White Herb** instantly restores the drop *and* is consumed → **Unburden doubles its Speed**, making a middling-Speed Sneasler suddenly outrun the field. **Coaching** then hands **Baxcalibur +1 Atk / +1 Def** — "the most disgusting thing ever" next to the tank. This is the same Unburden payoff the topic reaches two other ways: **Psychic Seed** in [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-baxcalibur-terrain-team|Chill's Baxcalibur team]] and **Swagger + Lum** in the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-banette-swagger-team|Mega Banette team]].
- **Gholdengo (Choice Scarf, Good as Gold).** The fast cleaner — a **Choice-Scarf** special breaker with **Power Gem** specifically for the Fire/Flying/Bug picks, plus **Make It Rain / Shadow Ball**. Good as Gold shuts off enemy status. *(Note: Make It Rain is a spread move that self-drops Sp. Atk by 2 in Champions — fire it and let the Scarf move you along.)*
- **Hisuian Arcanine (Rock Head, Focus Sash).** The fast finisher — **Rock Head** makes **Head Smash** and **Flare Blitz** recoil-free, so it hits "like a Choice Band" while holding a **Focus Sash** to survive one hit. **Extreme Speed** is the priority mop-up, and **Close Combat** the coverage (the creator's pick here — most Champions Arcanine run **Protect** in that slot instead). It's the team's answer to Kingambit and the fast threats a slow core can't chase.

## Game plan

1. **Lead the terrain + Fake Out.** Rillaboom sets Grassy Terrain and Fake-Outs; the heal keeps the bulky core alive and Grassy Glide gives you priority.
2. **Bring Farigiraf into Trick Room / Expanding-Force teams — every time.** If you see Indeedee / Hatterene / Mimikyu, lead or hold **Farigiraf** and **Imprison** their Trick Room away. The creator's losses came from *not* doing this.
3. **Set up Baxcalibur with Coaching.** Sneasler procs Unburden (Close Combat → White Herb), then **Coaches Baxcalibur** to +1/+1 so it tanks and swings even harder.
4. **Close with speed.** Hisuian Arcanine's Head Smash / Extreme Speed and Choice-Scarf Gholdengo clean up what the tank softens; Ice Shard handles priority-range dragons.

## Watch-outs (honest notes)

- **Launch-week ladder team, mixed results, self-admitted misplays.** He won two shown games and lost the third, and is candid ("I played this last one so bad," "I got bodied"). It's an early-meta build with strong tech, not a proven list. Moves/items/abilities are well-sourced; **EVs/natures are reconstructions.**
- **Mega Golisopod + Expanding Force is the recurring problem — for *both* Baxcalibur teams.** Like [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-baxcalibur-terrain-team|Chill's build]], this team's shown loss was to a **Mega Golisopod** team, here paired with **Indeedee + Hatterene Expanding-Force** under Trick Room. There's a clean mechanical reason it's a bad matchup: **Golisopod's Iron Head (Steel) is 2× on Baxcalibur's Ice**, and the opponent's **Psychic Terrain overwrites your Grassy Terrain** — erasing the heal *and* Grassy Glide's priority. It's a fair signal that launch-week Mega Baxcalibur cores are soft to Golisopod / psychic-spam — and the fix on *this* team is literally in the box (Farigiraf's Imprison), if you bring it.
- **Imprison only works while Farigiraf is on the field.** Lose it or switch it out and the opponent's Trick Room is available again — so protect the tech piece.
- **Baxcalibur's 4× Fire weakness still exists** (Dragon/Ice), and this team has no rain to soften it — Thermal Exchange stops the *burn* and turns Fire into an Attack boost, but a big Fire hit still hurts. (It also has no snow, so it lacks the +50% Defense Chill's version leans on.)
- **Sneasler's Unburden is a one-shot setup.** The White Herb triggers once; if Sneasler is forced to Close Combat into a bad spot or gets revenge-killed before it Coaches, the payoff is lost.

## Key Takeaways

- **Same tank, opposite shell.** Mega Baxcalibur is the standard Ice Shard / Glaive Rush / High Horsepower / Protect brick in both topic builds; the *teams* diverge completely — **Grassy Terrain + Imprison anti-TR + Rock-Head Arcanine** here vs **Psychic Terrain + snow + Expanding-Force spam** in [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-baxcalibur-terrain-team|Chill's]]. Studying the pair teaches how much a "build-around" is really about the *around*.
- **Imprison Farigiraf is dedicated anti-Trick-Room tech.** A Farigiraf that knows **Imprison + Trick Room** uses Imprison to **deny the opponent Trick Room** (and any other shared move) for as long as it stays in — the counter to the Indeedee/Hatterene/Mimikyu teams that punish a slow Baxcalibur.
- **The topic's third Unburden-Sneasler trigger** — **White Herb** (via Close Combat's own stat drop) here, vs **Psychic Seed** and **Swagger + Lum** elsewhere. Same doubled-Speed payoff, three different keys.
- **Grassy Terrain is a durability + priority layer** — the heal keeps the bulky core alive, it halves Earthquake, and Grassy Glide gives Rillaboom priority.
- **Honest launch-week caveat** — a fun, tech-forward early-Reg-M-C build the creator plays imperfectly; its loss (again to **Mega Golisopod + Expanding Force**) is the matchup to respect, and the fix is bringing the Farigiraf it already owns.
