So the tournament-proven Steelix team ([[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-steelix-wolfey-two-mode-team|#3]]) is a *finalist* — but the team that actually **beat** it, and won the event, is the Sun team below. ## The matchup — two wars decided the finals

**1. Weather war (sun vs sand).** Arsal's Charizard-Y sets **sun**; Wolfey's Tyranitar sets **sand** — and each Mega can *reset* the weather on evolving. Tyranitar is a double answer: it removes sun **and** its Rock Slide threatens Charizard. This is why Wolfey's Tyranitar is the linchpin against a sun team.

**2. Speed war (Trick Room vs Chlorophyll).** In sun, Venusaur's Chlorophyll doubles its Speed → it flies. Wolfey's counter is **Trick Room** (Mega Steelix mode), which *inverts* that speed. The twist: **both teams carry Sinistcha Trick Room** — so if Wolfey instead went Dragon-Dance-Tyranitar ("Queen") mode, Arsal's Sinistcha could flip Trick Room and turn Wolfey's built-up Speed against him. That mind-game is why the mode choice matters.

**Game 1 decision (a real "enemy team → plan" example):** Wolfey led **Steelix + Tyranitar** → committing to **Mega Steelix Trick Room mode**: Trick Room neutralizes Chlorophyll, Steelix walls Garchomp's physical Earthquake, and Tyranitar's sand handles Charizard. Textbook application of the [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-steelix-wolfey-two-mode-team|#3 decision guide]] — pick Trick-Room-Steelix vs a fast sun/offense team.

## Why this matters for the "enemy team → plan" tool (the honest half)

The prior examples show how to *pilot* the Steelix team; this one shows the **other half a real tool must give you: when you're on the losing side of a matchup.** Wolfey read the matchup correctly and still lost the finals — because team-vs-team, the sun build had the edge (Floette's Fairy Aura offense + Chlorophyll speed + its own Trick Room to contest Wolfey's). A genuinely useful "paste the enemy team → get a plan" feature ([[pokesynergy-niche-business-tool/hireui-translation]]) wouldn't just hand you a line — it would also flag **"this matchup is unfavorable; here's your best-case plan and its risk,"** which is exactly the *explainable, honest* recommender the operator wants (and the same discipline as the hireui candidate ADR: surface the tradeoff, don't hide it).

**Meta observation:** *both* finalists ran the **"two Megas, two modes, pick by enemy"** structure (Wolfey: Steelix/Tyranitar; Arsal: Charizard-Y/Floette). The adaptive-flexibility archetype from [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-steelix-wolfey-two-mode-team|#3]] wasn't a one-off — it was the *shape of the top of the meta* at this event.

## Key Takeaways

- **The Steelix team lost the finals** — it's a strong finalist, but **Arsal's Sun team (Mega Charizard-Y + Mega Floette) was the winning answer.** Don't over-read "COUNTERED the meta."
- **Two wars decided it:** sand-vs-sun (Tyranitar is Wolfey's key) and Trick-Room-vs-Chlorophyll (both ran Sinistcha TR).
- **Both finalists = two-Mega, two-mode, matchup-adaptive** — that structure is the top-meta shape, validating [[pokemon-champions-team-building/mega-steelix-wolfey-two-mode-team|#3]].
- **The tool lesson:** a real "enemy team → plan" feature must also tell you when the matchup is *bad* — honest tradeoffs, not just a confident line.
