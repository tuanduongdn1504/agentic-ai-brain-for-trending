# Demos & Kimi Work

The video spends its longest stretch (~[17:33]–[28:35] and [34:35]–end) on a reel of demos. They are **real capability showcases** — but almost all are **Moonshot's own first-party marketing**, not independent evaluations (UPHELD).

## The demo reel (all first-party unless noted)

- **Games (Moonshot showcases):** a Three.js/WebGPU procedural 3D open world; a Game Boy Advance emulator (real mGBA WASM core); a cyberpunk web-swinging game (Euler-pendulum physics, 120 FPS); a voxel Roman coliseum iterated over **20 rounds using headless-Chrome screenshot inspection**; a 4v4 FPS arena; a wuxia RPG built with Blender assets.
- **A user demo (not Moonshot):** "Chris GPT" — a CS:GO-style clone "three-shotted" for **~600K tokens / $3.24**, vs a claimed $10 (Fable 5) / $6 (GPT-5.6). Single anecdotal tweet; unverified magnitudes.
- **Interstellar's Gargantua** visualization — "working via watching itself from 62 screenshots."
- **Chip design:** a nano-model chip designed/optimized/verified in a 24–48h autonomous run on open-source EDA tools (Nangate 45nm). First-party.
- **Coding-for-research:** reproduced a computational-astrophysics workflow (20+ papers, 300+ equations of state, 3,000+ lines of Python, interactive dashboard) in ~2h vs a claimed 1–2 weeks. First-party.
- **Kimi Work (co-work):** interactive research reports — a 42-year AI-ASIC industry site (120 refinement rounds), a fusion-industry deck, a gravitational-wave analysis (391 events, 20+ concurrent sub-agents, 7 visualizations). First-party.

## How to read them

- **The multimodal feedback loop is the genuinely interesting mechanism:** K3 renders output, *looks* at it (screenshots), and iterates — "acting like a real developer." This is consistent with the confirmed native-multimodal architecture ([[architecture]]) and is the plausible source of its frontend-arena strength.
- **But they are marketing.** Moonshot's blog explicitly presents these as capability showcases / first-party case studies. They demonstrate a **ceiling under favorable conditions**, hand-picked, with external assets ("special thanks to Nintendo," "animated cowboy/horse models," "animations pack"). They are **not** independent, adversarial, or reproducible benchmarks.
- **They are also exactly what the skeptics warn about:** visually-impressive frontend/game demos are the category these models are most optimized for — and the least predictive of real-codebase robustness ([[reception-and-skeptics]]). The host even airs this critique, then moves on.

## Kimi Work vs the "cowork" pattern

- Kimi Work is Moonshot's agentic **co-work** surface (like ChatGPT's work mode) — long-horizon, sub-agent-orchestrated document/research production. It's the same product category the corpus tracks in [[../cowork-third-party-inference/_index]] and [[../claude-cowork/_index]], now from a Chinese lab.

## Key Takeaways

- The demos are **real and genuinely capable** — the screenshot-driven self-iteration loop is the standout mechanism.
- **Discount them as evidence of "most powerful":** first-party, curated, favorable-condition marketing — a capability *ceiling*, not a reliability *floor*.
- The gap between "look what it can do in this demo" and "will it fix a real bug in your codebase" is precisely where [[benchmarks-fact-vs-hype]] and [[reception-and-skeptics]] land: strong ceiling, shakier floor.
