# Hooks & Curiosity: Five Formulas to Stop the Scroll

**A hook opens by making the reader feel this message is *for them* (on-target specificity) while yanking forward with an unresolved question (curiosity loop), leveraging the brain's intolerance of gaps to force the next line.**

## Hook Anatomy: On-Target + Curiosity Loop

### The Two-Part Structure

A hook succeeds only when both parts engage:

- **On-target (specificity).** Reader recognizes themselves in the opening. Not "people who want income" but "Vietnamese students sitting in 12 m² dorm rooms at 11 PM, eating 15,000đ rice, calling mom for 2 million VND because shame of poverty." The more specific the context (where, when, who, exact pain), the more readers pause scrolling and think: *this is about me.*
- **Curiosity loop (information gap).** After the on-target context, a question or statement left incomplete. The brain cannot tolerate unresolved questions. Incomplete thought: "Until I found a video with a title that changed everything..." Reader *must* read next line to close the gap.

### Why Specificity Triggers Recognition

- Vague: "students 18–25 wanting to earn money online, lacking funds, seeking financial freedom"
- Specific: "students about to graduate, worried about their exam scores determining their job prospects. Already tried part-time, tutoring, café work, delivery. Know it generates income but feel exhausted, earn too little. Research online, search 'earn money online, passive income.' Obsessed with exam failure anxiety."

Vivid context—room dimensions, meal cost, air-con setting, time of night, emotional state—triggers recognition. The reader's amygdala fires: *I know this person. I am this person.* Conversion probability increases non-linearly.

## Five Hook Formulas

### Formula 1: News Effect

- Signal novelty, secrecy, or disruption.
- Structure: "New/Secret/Shocking + specific claim."
- Example: "The AI industry just published one metric nobody talks about: Vietnam ranks #10 globally for AI-generated-content views on YouTube—1.69 billion views. Here's why human copywriting now earns 3× more than it did in 2024."
- Psychology: **Novelty breaks pattern-matching.** Reader's RAS (reticular activating system) snaps attention to data deviation.

### Formula 2: Question Existing Truth

- Challenge a belief reader holds as fact.
- Structure: "Reverse common wisdom + offer alternative."
- Example: "You've been told cold email requires 700–1,000 emails for 1 customer. What if that number is wrong because your emails lack specificity, not volume?"
- Psychology: **Cognitive dissonance.** Conflicting belief and data force processing. Reader engages to resolve the tension.

### Formula 3: Enhance Pain or Desire

- Describe the unresolved struggle or future dream in vivid, specific terms.
- Structure: "Current pain + emotional consequence + contrast to ideal state."
- Example: "Every month you transfer money to your parents and feel the shame crawl up your spine. You know freelancing generates income, but 70 Upwork proposals per 1 win feels impossible. What if one skill compressed that 70× ratio down to 4–5?"
- Psychology: **Emotional identification.** Specificity + feeling creates stakes. Reader *feels* the problem is urgent.

### Formula 4: New Perspective

- Reframe the problem in an unexpected lens.
- Structure: "Old frame (wrong) + new frame (liberating) + reframe consequence."
- Example: "You've been thinking of email copywriting as 'writing.' It's actually 'psychology design'—80% research into your customer's pain, 20% arranging words. Once you see it this way, mediocre writers outpace 'talented' writers who skip the research phase."
- Psychology: **Aha moment.** New lens makes the path suddenly walkable. Reader feels permission to try.

### Formula 5: Reframe Problem as Opportunity

- Invert the obstacle into a gateway.
- Structure: "Obstacle (shared) + inversion (unexpected benefit) + access path."
- Example: "Your main disadvantage as a Vietnamese copywriter is that you're not a native English speaker. Turns out, that's your superpower: your writing avoids the AI-generated sentence structures English natives default to. Global clients pay 2–3× premium for copy that feels human."
- Psychology: **Agency restoration.** Reader shifts from victim (helpless) to strategist (lever-finder). Energy lifts.

## Information Gap Principle: Why Curiosity Cannot Be Ignored

### The Core Mechanism

- **Human brain is gap-intolerant.** An unresolved question creates cognitive dissonance. The brain *cannot rest* until the question closes. Reader *must* read the next line.
- **Cliffhanger vs. clickbait distinction.** A cliffhanger leaves info gap *unresolved but promised to resolve*. Clickbait creates gap with no intention to resolve (lying). Copywriting uses cliffhanger: "Here's what nobody tells you about..." → reader assumes resolution will follow.

### Gap-Closing Examples

- **Shocking statement leaves gap:** "11 PM in a 12 m² dorm room in Saigon, eating 15,000đ rice, AC set to 35°C because I couldn't afford the electricity. [Gap: why is this relevant?] Until I found a video about copywriting and earned 20M VND/month in 6 months. [Gap closes.]"
- **Contradiction creates gap:** "I really hate your video. [Gap: author seems hostile?] Because it fascinated me so much I watched it 3 times. [Gap resolves as backhanded compliment.]"
- **Promised reveal:** "Research is 80% of copywriting. But here's what 95% of beginners skip in that research phase..." [Gap: what's the skip?] Reader reads on to find out.

### The Paradox: Curiosity Loop is Load-Bearing

Without curiosity loop, on-target specificity alone fails:
- "Students from Saigon facing exam anxiety." (Specific, but no forward momentum.)
- "Students from Saigon facing exam anxiety. Until I discovered one skill that changed everything." (Specific + gap = scroll-stop.)

The gap *compels* the read. The on-target makes the reader *care enough to want the read.*

## Specificity Over Vagueness: Signals That Create Conversion

### Vagueness (Dies on Arrival)

- "My customer is a student wanting income."
- "They feel unmotivated."
- "They need a solution to earn more."

**Result:** Reader scrolls. Too generic. Could apply to 100M people.

### Specificity (Sticks in the Mind)

- "My customer is a Vietnamese student on the cliff edge of graduation. Tried part-time work, tutoring, delivery. Each pays 15,000–25,000đ/hour. Exhausted. Desperate. Calling home for 2M VND/month in shame."

**Result:** Reader pauses. Feels seen. Emotionally activated.

### The Signal Conversion

- **Vivid context** (room size, meal cost, AC temp, time of night) = reader *visualizes* the scene, activating memory/mirror-neuron systems.
- **Specific emotional state** (shame, exhaustion, desperation) = reader's amygdala recognizes the pain they carry.
- **Concrete behaviors** (called home, asked for money, tried freelance) = reader sees themselves in the actions, not just circumstances.

**Principle:** Vagueness *hopes* the reader self-identifies. Specificity *confirms* they already have.

## Avoid: Fake Curiosity, Misleading Headlines, AI-Heavy Punctuation

### Anti-Pattern 1: Fake Curiosity (Clickbait)

- **Bad:** "You won't BELIEVE what I found in my email." [Gap created but no resolution promised—reader feels manipulated.]
- **Good:** "I found a pattern in 500 email sequences that explains why 80% of them fail on the first read. Here's what you're missing..." [Gap + promised resolution = authentic curiosity.]

**Test:** Does the next sentence *resolve* the gap you created, or does it deflect to another gap? Authentic copy resolves; clickbait re-creates gaps to extend engagement artificially.

### Anti-Pattern 2: Misleading Headlines

- **Bad:** "Earn $10,000/month in 3 weeks." [Promise never realized; course teaches 4 months minimum and income depends on customer acquisition strategy.]
- **Good:** "How I went from 5M VND/month to 20M VND/month in 6 months using one framework." [Specific, time-scoped, teachable.]

**Test:** Can the course *actually deliver* what the headline claims? If no, it's misleading. The course teaches $0 → $1,000/month over 4+ months with deliberate practice + customer acquisition effort, not guaranteed in a fixed timeline.

### Anti-Pattern 3: AI-Heavy Punctuation and Word Patterns

Readers reject copy that *feels* AI-generated. Signals include:

- **Em-dashes overuse:** "The secret to copywriting—which nobody talks about—is research—specifically, deep research into customer psychology—and once you understand this, everything changes—absolutely everything—about your writing."
  - **Fix:** Use em-dashes sparingly. Break into shorter sentences. Use rhythm variation.

- **Excessive commas and semicolons:** "The process, in essence, is threefold: first, you research; second, you draft; third, you edit—each phase, crucial and non-negotiable, compounds the previous one."
  - **Fix:** Simplify sentence structure. Use natural speech rhythm. Fewer modifiers.

- **Inflated adjectives stacked:** "This groundbreaking, transformative, revolutionary approach will fundamentally, positively, and dramatically revolutionize your entire existence."
  - **Fix:** One strong verb. One strong adjective. "This approach will change your life" reads more human than inflated framing.

- **No personal voice or detail:** Copy that lists benefits without personal story, without humor, without mistakes, without "I"—feels corporate/bot-generated.
  - **Fix:** Write as you speak. Include personal struggle. Share failure. Use customer language. Add natural sound (laughter, pauses, real emotions).

### The Consistency Test

Read your hook aloud. Does it *sound* like a human wrote it, or like a prompt-engineering output? 

- Human writing includes: specific mistakes, personal details, rhythm variation, natural profanity or colloquialism, pauses (periods, short sentences), humor, emotional rawness.
- AI writing includes: perfect grammar, generic metaphors, predictable structure, inflated language, no stumbles, no personality risks.

**Principle:** Imperfect human copy outconverts perfect AI copy in every tested metric because readers trust authenticity over polish.

## Connection to Broader Copywriting Frameworks

- **Five Stages of Persuasion:** Hook is Stage 1 (Awaken desire/pain). Without hook, reader never reaches Stages 2–5.
- **Storytelling Golden Structure (6-Point):** Hook sets up context + crisis. The 6-point story opens with specificity and emotional stakes—a refined hook.
- **Email Templates (PAS, BAB, Educational):** Each template opens with a hook. PAS opens "Problem (on-target pain)." BAB opens "Before (specific struggle)." Educational opens "Misconception (curiosity gap)."
- **Writing Process (Research 80%, Edit 20%):** Hook specificity comes from *research*, not inspiration. Knowing customer context precisely is how you write authentic on-target openings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hook = On-Target + Curiosity Loop.** Specificity (on-target) makes reader feel included; unresolved question (curiosity) compels forward reading. Both are non-negotiable.
- **Five formulas unlock pattern-matching:** News Effect, Question Existing Truth, Enhance Pain/Desire, New Perspective, Reframe Problem. Each breaks reader's habitual scrolling and forces attention.
- **Information gap is neurological fact.** Human brain cannot rest on unresolved questions. Write gaps into your copy, promise resolution, deliver it. Gap-closing = compelled reading.
- **Specificity converts.** Vivid context (room size, meal cost, time, emotion, behavior) triggers recognition and mirror-neuron activation. Vagueness dies on arrival.
- **Claway and others prove hooks scale.** Course cites Claway (350K subscribers, 77 videos) as proof, but specific metrics are unverified; use as inspiration for pattern-testing, not outcome guarantee.
- **Avoid fake curiosity, misleading claims, AI-heavy punctuation.** Authentic hooks resolve their gaps; human writing *sounds* like speech and includes personality risks AI avoids. Imperfect authenticity outconverts perfect polish.
