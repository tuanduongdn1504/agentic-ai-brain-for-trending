## §2 — Core Scrum knowledge *(you're PSM II — this is crisp interview phrasing)*

**What is Scrum?** A lightweight framework for delivering value through **empiricism** (learn by doing) and **iterative, incremental** delivery. Built on 3 pillars — **Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation.**

**The 5 Scrum Values:** **Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage.** *(PSM interviewers love these — weave them in.)*

**The Scrum Team (3 accountabilities):** **Product Owner** (maximizes product value, owns the Product Backlog), **Scrum Master** (accountable for the team's effectiveness + Scrum being understood/enacted), **Developers** (build the Increment). One team, no sub-teams (Scrum Guide 2020).

**5 Events (all inspect-and-adapt):**
- **The Sprint** — the container, ≤1 month, fixed length.
- **Sprint Planning** — *why* (Sprint Goal), *what* (backlog items), *how* (plan).
- **Daily Scrum** — 15 min, Developers re-plan toward the Sprint Goal (not a status report to the SM).
- **Sprint Review** — inspect the Increment with stakeholders, adapt the Product Backlog.
- **Sprint Retrospective** — inspect how the team worked, commit to improvements.

**3 Artifacts + their commitments:** Product Backlog → **Product Goal**; Sprint Backlog → **Sprint Goal**; Increment → **Definition of Done.**

**The Scrum Master is a servant-leader, NOT a boss or a PM.** You *serve* the team (coach, facilitate, remove impediments), the PO (backlog techniques, clarity), and the organization (adopting Scrum). You **don't** assign tasks, command, or report on individuals.

**Common "do you really get it?" checks:**
- *Daily Scrum is for the Developers, not a status update to you.* ✅
- *You don't "give story points" — the Developers estimate.* ✅
- *Velocity is a planning aid, not a productivity KPI or a stick.* ✅
- *You facilitate the Retro; the team owns the improvements.* ✅
- *Definition of Done = quality; the team can't call work "done" without it.* ✅
- **Agile vs Scrum vs Kanban:** Agile = the mindset (values/principles); Scrum = a framework (roles/events/artifacts, timeboxed sprints); Kanban = flow/WIP-limits, continuous. Know when each fits.

## §3 — Scenario & behavioral questions *(anchor to YOUR real stories)*

**Q. A team member keeps skipping / disengaging from the Daily Scrum.** *(You have this exact story.)*
→ *"I talk to them privately first to understand the blocker — not call them out in front of the team. I reframe the Daily as the team's tool, not a report to me — 'we rise together and fail together,' transparency on yesterday/today/blockers. I coach, I don't force. In my Innovation team I had this and the private, curious conversation turned it around."*

**Q. Two team members are in conflict.**
→ *"Listen to both separately, separate the person from the problem, bring it back to a shared goal and the Sprint Goal, facilitate a decision on the project's terms, and document the agreement. My job is to make the conflict productive, not to take a side."*

**Q. The Product Owner keeps pushing new scope mid-Sprint.**
→ *"I protect the Sprint Goal — the team needs focus. I coach the PO that mid-sprint changes threaten the commitment, and we handle new items through refinement for the next sprint, unless it's critical enough to renegotiate the Sprint Goal transparently with the Developers."*

**Q. The team keeps missing its Sprint commitments.**
→ *"I treat it empirically — inspect in the Retro. Usual root causes: over-forecasting, unclear Definition of Done, hidden dependencies, or interruptions. I facilitate the team to find the cause and pick one improvement, and I track whether it worked next sprint. I don't blame; I make the problem visible."*

**Q. How do you improve a low-maturity team? (they explicitly want 'team maturity')**
→ *"Start with transparency — make the work and the impediments visible. Then coach the ceremonies until they're genuinely useful, not ritual. Build psychological safety through the Retro. Grow the team from me facilitating everything to the team self-organizing. My Innovation team went from waterfall to a working Agile cadence this way."*

**Q. A manager wants you to report who's slow / make you a task-master.**
→ *"I'd educate, kindly. A Scrum Master serves the team's effectiveness, not surveillance — individual policing kills the transparency that makes Scrum work. I'd offer team-level metrics and outcomes instead, and coach the manager toward trusting the team."*

**Your real proof points to deploy:** the **waterfall→Agile Innovation-team transformation**; **coaching a colleague to PSM II**; running the **internship + mentorship program** (recruiting/training/coaching); **PO collaboration** on backlog + stakeholder value; driving Agile delivery of **two shipped products** at CVTOT.
