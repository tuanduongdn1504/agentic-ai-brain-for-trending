**The resolution is not to retire 23 rows.** The deferrals were right about the substance: those rows are not dead weight. Each records a verified corpus-first with its evidence, and several have been cited by later ships as prior art. Retiring them would destroy the catalogue that makes collision-detection possible — which is exactly how the v182 audit caught the #23 anchor error.

**So the rule was wrong, not the practice.** §C was defined as *"live standalone candidates"* — a promotion queue. In fact it has been doing two jobs: holding genuine promotion candidates, and cataloguing one-off corpus-firsts. §39's auto-retire is correct for the first job and destructive for the second.

**DECISION — §C is bifurcated** (routine v2.8 §44):

| **§C-1** |  live promotion candidates, **N ≥ 2** | **11** | applies | a real queue; a second instance means the class may be recurring |
| **§C-2** | recorded corpus-firsts, **N = 1** | **38** | **does not apply** | a collision-detection catalogue; retained for evidential value, explicitly **not** awaiting promotion |

**Consequences, stated plainly:**

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ The method contribution: the same-author control (routine v2.8 §42)

**What happened.** v256, v257 and v258 were three repositories by one author (`mranex`), shipped consecutively at the operator's request: manga/image → light novels/text → video/subtitles. By the third, the *comparison* had become more valuable than any of the three subjects.

**What it produced that no single ship could:**

- **Six bookkeeping habits replicating across all three**, by *different mechanisms* each time — most sharply, ignored-yet-tracked files arriving via three distinct causal routes (rule and exception in one commit; files 26 days before the rule; the rule **deleted** by the commit that added the files). An outcome invariant across three mechanisms is evidence of a **disposition**, which one repository cannot establish.
- **A measurable correlation on the diverging axes:** the repository with the most written-down reasoning (v258 — four planning documents, one surviving) was the best-built; the one with none (v257 — never had a planning document) was the worst-built. ⚠️ **N=3 from one author is a hypothesis, not a law, and the ships said so.**
- **A generalisation with a mechanism:** *code moves forward in time and never backward.* An alignment guard invented in v256 on 2026-05-15 reached v258 eleven days later and never reached v257 — which was edited six times after that date. Copy-forward is a one-way ratchet, and **the only mechanism that makes a fix travel backwards is a shared library.**
- **A rule:** *a plan document is a fence around exactly the surfaces it names* — measured, in v258, by which shared layers were imported and which were copy-pasted.

**Adopted as §42.** When the operator requests consecutive subjects by one author, the ship should (a) treat the **comparison** as a first-class deliverable, (b) hold a habit table across the set, distinguishing **dispositions** from **attention**, (c) verify each row with commands in *every* clone rather than assuming symmetry — both of this audit's parent ships were corrected on exactly that error — and (d) state the sample size and refuse to generalise past it.

⭐ **And the finding turns on the vault.** `05 Skills/` holds skills copied and re-versioned rather than shared; the routine exists as v2.1 → v2.8 in separate files. **A fix made in the newest copy will not reach the older ones, for precisely the reason it did not reach v257.** That is the operator-facing item this audit hands forward.
